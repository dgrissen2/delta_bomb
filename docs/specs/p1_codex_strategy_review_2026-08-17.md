## Codex Strategy Review — Generic Logic
**Verdict**: FAIL (12 findings)

### Findings

| # | Finding | Severity | Category | Details |
|---|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| 1 | The supplied evidence cannot establish fidelity to Pandar’s NVDA book | HIGH | Strategy fidelity | The supplied white paper concerns SPX, 20–40 DTE, roughly one-standard-deviation strikes, minute-scale legging, and prompt closure of failed placements. It does not document this NVDA, 5–12 DTE, approximately 4-delta, multiday naked-short process. The cited PMTraders reconstruction is not substantiated by the supplied context, so results can support only this mechanized variant—not conclusions about what Pandar actually did. |
| 2 | Signal and entry timing create look-ahead bias | CRITICAL | Backtest validity | The trigger uses closing ORATS values but assumes entry at 15:55 on the same session. Those closing values were not then observable. ThetaData EOD quotes likewise are not necessarily the 15:55 executable NBBO. The signal must use timestamped pre-entry data or trade no earlier than the next session. |
| 3 | The trigger is disconnected from the option being traded | HIGH | Trigger logic | A constant-maturity 10-day, fixed-5-delta surface point can behave differently from the selected listed expiry and 2–6-delta contract. Fixed-delta series also migrate across strikes as spot moves. Consequently, measured “wing crush” need not produce a gain or buyback fill in the original option. |
| 4 | Threshold fitting optimizes the wrong objective and invites overfitting | HIGH | Optimization | Maximizing five-session surface wing-crush does not maximize executable P&L, buyback probability, or tail-adjusted return; spot movement can overwhelm IV compression. The 85/70 baseline, 12-signals-per-year floor, candidate-search space, and flat-maximum rule lack derivation. Clustered daily signals are treated as independent observations, and the test-window sensitivity grid adds selection risk. |
| 5 | Instrument selection does not define one reproducible strategy | HIGH | Instrument design | Approximately 4 delta and the 25%-OTM fallback are economically different rules, while the requested EOD dataset does not guarantee the greeks needed to determine which rule applies. “One listed strike nearer” produces variable widths, not consistently $5. Pooling those trades without width or risk normalization confounds instrument choice with data availability and listing conventions. |
| 6 | Buyback and fill mechanics can generate impossible or misleading outcomes | HIGH | Execution | Entry permits a $0.05 bid while baseline retained credit is $0.10, producing a nonpositive buy limit. Orders begin next session despite being described as rested immediately. Filling every qualifying order at its limit makes “median credit kept” tautologically equal to the chosen constant. A close-ask rule misses intraday fills that change the position from naked to defined-risk, not merely its execution price. |
| 7 | The fill-bias validation cannot validate the historical backtest | HIGH | Validation | May–August 2026 cannot establish fill behavior across the distinct 2023–2025 regimes, and a scalar caveat cannot propagate nonlinear changes in fill status and tail exposure. The stated 171 sessions between 2026-05-04 and 2026-08-14 is chronologically impossible. Even one-minute ask touches require assumptions about quote duration, displayed size, queue position, and whether the order was live. |
| 8 | The “free spread” omits its economic opportunity cost | HIGH | Counterfactual | When the nearer option reaches the limit, the farther short should normally cost less to repurchase. Closing that short would therefore retain more cash than buying the nearer option. The difference is the economic cost of keeping the vertical. Reporting its later payoff without comparison to same-time buy-to-close can make an inferior transformation appear free. |
| 9 | Naked-call tail risk invalidates the proposed sizing rule | CRITICAL | Tail risk | Put loss at zero is bounded, but naked-call loss is unbounded; “NVDA doubles” is only one scenario, not notional at risk. A 10%-NLV allowance per trade ignores overlapping expiries, simultaneous sides, overnight gaps, margin expansion, forced liquidation, assignment, and correlated exposure. Excluding sizing and margin survival from pass/fail permits promotion of a book that may be impossible to hold through the modeled loss path. |
| 10 | P&L and fee normalization mix incompatible risk units | HIGH | Accounting | Dividing pre-split premiums by ten while multiplying the contract multiplier by ten preserves one legacy contract’s dollars, but that contract represents ten post-split contracts’ exposure. “Per-contract” results therefore overweight pre-split trades and make regime comparisons and account scaling invalid. Fees must also be defined per executed leg and normalized consistently; cash-intrinsic settlement assumes away physical assignment and funding constraints. |
| 11 | The pass/hold/stop gates do not establish viability | CRITICAL | Decision logic | The 60% fill hurdle, half-median-income loss allowance, three-of-four-regime rule, and sign-flip condition have no economic or statistical basis. “Trailing-12-month median income” is undefined, regimes are unequal and retrospectively chosen, and 2026 intraday coverage cannot determine fill-induced sign changes in earlier regimes. Side-versus-combined application and decision precedence are ambiguous, allowing a catastrophic call side or one dominant loss regime to be hidden. |
| 12 | The design lacks the falsification needed for a promotion decision | HIGH | Falsification | Positive returns could be generic short-volatility carry rather than signal skill. Required controls include matched nonsignal dates, unconditional and randomized entries, same-option buy-to-close, immediate defined-risk verticals, naked-to-expiry, alternative strike rules, known Pandar examples, leave-one-shock-out analysis, block-bootstrap uncertainty, and explicit overnight gap and margin-liquidation stresses. Roughly 3.6 observed years in one preselected stock cannot estimate a 4-delta short’s ruin risk. |

## Codex Strategy Review — Charlie McElligott
**Verdict**: FAIL (12 findings)

### Persona Lens

This lens treats wing richness as evidence of forced positioning, not generic mispricing. It notices that a one-sided upside grab, a downside liquidation, and a two-sided post-shock smile create different dealer-gamma feedback loops and fail in opposite directions. Averaging them together can conceal the rare short-gamma loss that determines the book’s survival.

### Findings

| # | Finding | Severity | Category | Details |
|---|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| 1 | Signal and entry timing contain look-ahead bias | CRITICAL | Backtest validity | The trigger is evaluated using day-*t* closing ORATS values, yet entry occurs at 15:55 on day *t*. Those closing values are not demonstrably observable before entry. ThetaData EOD quotes also are not necessarily executable 15:55 NBBOs. Use timestamped pre-entry inputs or execute the next session. |
| 2 | The supplied evidence does not establish fidelity to Pandar’s NVDA book | HIGH | Strategy fidelity | The white paper documents SPX, 20–40 DTE, roughly one-standard-deviation strikes, minute-scale legging, and managed failed placements—not NVDA 5–12 DTE, approximately 4-delta, multiday naked shorts. Put-side conversion is evidenced more clearly than a symmetric call-side buyback program. Results would validate this mechanized variant, not “what Pandar did.” |
| 3 | One trigger conflates mechanically different flow regimes | HIGH | Trigger logic | Wing and kink percentiles can fire during a one-sided spot-up/vol-up grab or a two-sided post-shock smile. The former exposes a short call to a dealer-gamma chase; the latter can trigger both sides and create a short strangle during instability. RR, spot direction, overall front IV, and opposite-wing state are merely tags, so the test pools trades with different causes and failure modes. |
| 4 | The trigger is disconnected from the contract traded | HIGH | Basis risk | A constant-maturity 10-day, fixed-5-delta surface point is not the selected listed 5–12 DTE, 2–6-delta option. Fixed-delta observations also migrate between strikes as spot moves. A measured wing crush therefore need not make the original option profitable or fill its buyback. |
| 5 | Threshold fitting optimizes the wrong outcome and invites overfitting | HIGH | Optimization | Maximizing five-session wing crush does not maximize executable P&L, buyback probability, or tail-adjusted return; spot movement can overwhelm IV compression. The 85/70 baseline, 12-signals-per-year floor, search domain, aggregation statistic, and flat-maximum rule lack economic or statistical derivation. Clustered daily signals further overstate effective sample size. |
| 6 | Instrument and order rules do not define one coherent strategy | HIGH | Instrument design | Approximately 4 delta and the 25%-OTM fallback are materially different exposures, while the acquisition plan does not guarantee historical Greeks. “One strike nearer” creates variable widths rather than a consistent $5 spread. Moreover, a permitted $0.05 sale with baseline `c = $0.10` produces a negative buy limit, making the baseline incompatible with Pandar’s nickel-tail examples. |
| 7 | The fill proxy cannot support a promotion decision | HIGH | Execution | The buy is described as rested immediately but becomes active only next session, excluding same-day reversals central to the mechanism. Close ask misses intraday and gap-open fills that change the position discontinuously from naked to defined-risk. May–August 2026 cannot validate execution across 2023–2025 regimes, and a scalar caveat cannot repair altered fill states or determine whether earlier regime signs flip. |
| 8 | The “free spread” omits its economic opportunity cost | HIGH | Counterfactual | At the proposed fill time, closing the cheaper far short would normally retain more cash than purchasing the nearer option. That foregone cash is the cost of the residual vertical. Without a same-time buy-to-close comparator, later spread payoffs can make an inferior profit-taking choice look free; “median credit kept” is also tautologically `c` under the fill rule. |
| 9 | Naked-call risk and book-level sizing are not controlled | CRITICAL | Tail risk | A put’s zero-price loss is bounded, but a naked call’s loss is not bounded by “NVDA doubles.” The 10%-NLV calculation is per trade and ignores overlapping expiries, simultaneous sides, gap-through-strike moves, volatility-driven margin expansion, forced liquidation, assignment, and accumulated inventory. Excluding PNR and margin survival from pass/fail allows promotion of a strategy that cannot survive its modeled path to expiry. |
| 10 | Split and fee normalization mix incompatible contract units | HIGH | Accounting | Dividing pre-split prices by ten while using a 10× multiplier preserves the dollars of one legacy contract, but that contract represents ten post-split contracts’ exposure. Pooling those observations as “per one contract” overweights pre-split regimes and invalidates account scaling. Fees must likewise be stated per executed leg and normalized to the same exposure unit. |
| 11 | The promotion gates are arbitrary and internally underdefined | CRITICAL | Decision logic | The 60% fill hurdle, half-median-income loss allowance, three-of-four-regime rule, and sign-flip test have no demonstrated economic or statistical basis. “Trailing-12-month median income” is undefined; regimes are unequal and retrospective; criteria are ambiguous between each side and the combined book. A catastrophic call side or one dominant loss regime can therefore be hidden by put income and averaging. |
| 12 | The design cannot falsify signal skill or estimate ruin risk | HIGH | Falsification | Positive results may be ordinary short-vol carry rather than wing-selection skill. Missing controls include unconditional and matched nonsignal sales, randomized dates, immediate verticals, same-option buy-to-close, naked-to-expiry, known Pandar examples, leave-one-shock-out tests, and block-bootstrap uncertainty. Roughly 3.6 years in one preselected stock cannot estimate the ruin probability of repeated 4-delta naked shorts. |

### Blind Spots And Missing Work

- Timestamped, executable signal and quote data—with quote size and order-state assumptions—across every test regime.
- Locked trade reconstructions from Pandar’s actual callouts, with put and call behavior tested separately.
- Portfolio-level PNR, margin, assignment, concurrent-short, and accumulated-spread-inventory simulation.
- Separate flow archetypes using spot direction, RR, opposite-wing state, front IV, and—where available—call/put OI and dealer-gamma concentration.
- Benchmarks, uncertainty intervals, shock-removal tests, and explicit overnight gap/liquidation scenarios.
- A prospective paper protocol with thresholds frozen in advance and promotion decided separately by side and archetype.

## Codex Strategy Review — Senior Quant Researcher
**Verdict**: FAIL (10 findings)

### Persona Lens

A generic reviewer may accept the strategy narrative. A quant reviewer notices that timestamp leakage, split-unit errors, non-tradable calibration targets, clustered observations, and omitted jump-risk economics can invalidate an otherwise plausible backtest.

### Findings

| # | Finding | Severity | Category | Details |
|---|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| 1 | Signal uses future information | CRITICAL | Temporal integrity | §1 evaluates the signal at day *t* close but executes at 15:55 on the same day. The closing ORATS surface is not available at 15:55. This directly contaminates every trade. Use a timestamped 15:55 surface or enter no earlier than *t+1*. |
| 2 | Split normalization creates a 10× exposure discontinuity | CRITICAL | Units / accounting | Dividing pre-split premiums by 10 and multiplying the contract multiplier by 10 preserves the P&L of one historical 100-share contract, but that contract represents ten post-split 100-share units. “Per-contract” P&L, fees, worst months, and sizing therefore become incomparable across 2024-06-10. Normalize to constant share/notional exposure and handle adjusted contracts separately. |
| 3 | The proposed trigger is not validated as Pandar’s trigger | HIGH | Construct validity | The 10-day 5Δ wing and 30-day kink ranks are newly invented proxies. They omit the documented expiry-specific richness, recent option-price behavior, spot/vol interaction, and distinct call-grab versus put-tail contexts. The supplied white paper cannot validate them because it concerns SPX, 20–40 DTE, approximately 1σ options, and minute-scale legging. A labeled set of Pandar entries and deliberate non-entries is needed. |
| 4 | The cooldown and exit rules do not simulate the book | HIGH | Strategy fidelity | §4 permits only one position per side/expiry while it remains open, apparently including a completed spread. That prevents repeated tail sales after naked risk has been removed—the mechanism by which the spread inventory was accumulated. Likewise, holding every completed spread to expiry while merely recording inflation opportunities omits monetization and rolling. Test a single-cycle strategy and the actual inventory-building book separately. |
| 5 | The fill proxy is structurally biased and cannot govern promotion | HIGH | Execution modeling | The buy order begins only next session, although it is supposedly rested immediately; close-ask fills miss opens and intraday touches. Validation covers only recent 2026 sessions and is applied merely as a caveat, while the biased EOD result still drives the 60% pass threshold. Calibrate both entry and buyback fills by regime, DTE, moneyness, and time of day, then require the decision to survive plausible fill models. |
| 6 | Instrument selection silently changes the hypothesis | HIGH | Instrument definition | A 4Δ contract and a 25%-OTM fallback are not equivalent; missing greeks would create a non-random strategy change. “One listed strike nearer” also produces materially different widths across price regimes and split-adjusted chains despite the $5-wide claim. Finally, bid ≥ $0.05 permits `L = sale − 0.10 ≤ 0`, making the baseline buyback impossible. Fix one invariant definition and require a positive, tick-valid limit. |
| 7 | Threshold fitting optimizes the wrong outcome | HIGH | Calibration | Maximizing five-session wing-IV compression does not maximize short-option P&L: IV can collapse while spot runs toward or through the strike. The objective also selects mean reversion in the same variable used to trigger, while “12 signals/year” ignores clustered, overlapping observations. Fit against executable trade P&L—or treat thresholds as pre-registered hypotheses—and cluster by independent tail-expansion episode. |
| 8 | The design cannot identify incremental edge | HIGH | Falsification | There is no mandatory comparison with the same short held naked, an immediately purchased vertical, matched non-signal dates, or a simple delta/DTE tail-sale rule. Positive results could be ordinary theta or variance-risk-premium harvesting rather than value from the trigger or buyback mechanism. These counterfactuals are necessary before attributing edge. |
| 9 | Naked-tail sizing is not a risk model | CRITICAL | Tail risk / feasibility | A 10%-of-NLV scenario per trade does not aggregate concurrent expiries or both sides, model dynamic NLV and broker margin, or prevent forced liquidation before expiry. “Stock doubles” is an arbitrary finite scenario for an unbounded short call. Cash-intrinsic settlement also removes assignment, pin, and after-hours stock risk. Paper promotion requires portfolio-level exposure, margin, jump/vol shocks, and expected-shortfall analysis. |
| 10 | Pass criteria are arbitrary and partly undefined | HIGH | Decision governance | The 60% fill rate is not tied to expectancy; “trailing-12-month median income” lacks a precise definition; four unequal, post hoc regimes are not independent samples; and a sign-only fill-bias test can still change the fill-rate verdict. “Baseline” is also ambiguous between 85/70 and the fitted pair. Require exact formulas, minimum effective sample sizes, confidence intervals, tail-loss limits, and separate side/archetype decisions. |

### Blind Spots And Missing Work

- Ground-truth reconstruction of actual Pandar trades and non-trades, especially asymmetric call behavior.
- Split-neutral exposure, transaction-level fees, portfolio margin, assignment, and overlapping-position accounting.
- Counterfactuals isolating trigger value, buyback value, and free-spread monetization value.
- Historical intraday calibration of entry and buyback execution beyond the 2026 sample.
- Block-bootstrap or episode-level uncertainty estimates, walk-forward validation, and synthetic jump/volatility stresses outside the short historical window.
