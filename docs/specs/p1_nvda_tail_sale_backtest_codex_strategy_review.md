## Codex Strategy Review — Generic Logic
**Verdict**: FAIL (12 findings)

### Findings

| # | Finding | Severity | Category | Details |
|---|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| 1 | Signal timing contains look-ahead | HIGH | Backtest validity | The trigger uses close-of-day ORATS data but assumes entry at 15:55 that same day. Those closing-surface values were not yet observable. ThetaData EOD bids also are not necessarily executable 15:55 bids. Compute the trigger from timestamped pre-entry data or execute on the next session. |
| 2 | The trigger does not measure richness of the option actually sold | HIGH | Trigger | A constant-maturity 10-day, fixed-5Δ surface can differ materially from the selected 5–12 DTE expiry and 2–6Δ contract. Fixed-delta wing changes also roll across strikes, so “wing crush” need not mean the original option became profitable. Symmetric put/call thresholds, no spot-direction condition, and acceptance of two-wing blowouts can generate sales opposite to Pandar’s side-specific judgment. |
| 3 | Threshold fitting optimizes a non-economic surrogate | HIGH | Optimization | Maximizing five-session surface wing-crush does not maximize buyback fills, short-option P&L, or portfolio utility; spot movement can overwhelm IV compression, particularly for calls. The surface-only fit also cannot apply executable-strike, liquidity, expiry, or fill constraints consistently. The 85/70 baseline and 12-signals-per-year floor remain unsupported judgment constants. |
| 4 | Instrument selection tests a materially different book | HIGH | Strategy fidelity | Pandar described black-swan-plausible strikes rather than fixed delta, multiple strikes and expiries, and weeklies extending toward the next monthly expiry. The spec instead selects one nearest 5–12 DTE expiry, one approximately 4Δ strike, and forbids further trades on that side/expiry. The 25%-OTM fallback is not equivalent to 4Δ, while “one listed strike nearer” does not reliably create the claimed $5-wide spread. |
| 5 | Some baseline buyback orders are impossible | HIGH | Execution | Entry permits a $0.05 bid while baseline `c` is $0.10, producing `L = sale − c ≤ 0`. Such orders cannot fill and will mechanically depress the principal pass metric. The order also starts next session despite the strategy saying it is rested immediately, omitting same-day reversals. “Median credit kept” is tautologically `c` because the model always fills at the limit. |
| 6 | The fill proxy cannot support the promotion decision | HIGH | Fill model | Close ask below the limit misses intraday and gap-open fills and therefore changes whether the simulation holds a spread or a naked short. Validation only during May–August 2026 cannot establish bias across 2023–2025 regimes, and a scalar caveat does not propagate that uncertainty into results. The claimed 171 sessions between May 4 and August 14 is chronologically impossible. One-minute ask touches also require quote-size and execution assumptions. |
| 7 | Naked-short survival risk is excluded from the decision gate | CRITICAL | Tail risk | Put loss at zero is bounded, but naked-call loss is unbounded; “NVDA doubles” is merely one scenario, not notional at risk. A 10%-NLV limit per trade ignores concurrent expiries, correlated positions, overnight gaps, assignment, changing portfolio-margin requirements, and forced covering. Keeping sizing outside pass/fail permits promotion of a strategy that cannot survive at its proposed scale. |
| 8 | Pass, hold, and stop criteria do not logically establish viability | CRITICAL | Decision logic | The 60% fill hurdle, half-median-income loss allowance, three-of-four-regime rule, and sign-flip test have no economic or statistical derivation. “Trailing-12-month median income” is undefined, regimes are unequal and selected retrospectively, and the 2026 fill study cannot determine signs in earlier regimes. Combined results can conceal a failing or catastrophic call side, while a slightly positive total can pass despite unacceptable expected shortfall or capital usage. |
| 9 | The purported test set is neither sufficient nor epistemically out of sample | HIGH | Statistical inference | Roughly 3.6 years in one stock contains too few independent observations to estimate the loss distribution of 4Δ weekly shorts. The spec is dated after the entire test window and names the shocks and regimes used for evaluation, so the window was already observed when thresholds and gates were chosen. Overlapping 12-month windows and a large sensitivity grid add further selection risk without confidence intervals or multiple-testing controls. |
| 10 | Required causal counterfactuals and falsification tests are absent | HIGH | Falsification | Positive returns could come from generic volatility risk premium rather than the wing trigger or nearer-strike purchase. The design needs matched non-signal dates, randomized/opposite-side signals, unconditional short-vol benchmarks, same-strike profit-taking, naked-to-expiry, and known Pandar positive and negative examples. Leave-one-shock-out and threshold-stability tests are also necessary to show the conclusion is not episode-dependent. |
| 11 | P&L normalization does not represent deployable economics | HIGH | Accounting | Dividing pre-split strikes and premiums by ten while multiplying the contract multiplier by ten preserves the dollar P&L of one old contract, but gives it ten times the exposure of one post-split contract. Summed “per-contract” results therefore compare inconsistent risk units. Fees are not defined per executed leg, and the model omits aggregate margin use, return on capital, assignment/exercise effects, and portfolio-level drawdown. |
| 12 | P1 is only a stylized component of Pandar’s actual book | HIGH | Scope | The supplied white paper describes SPX, 20–40 DTE, approximately 1σ options and minute-scale legging—not this NVDA program. Pandar’s actual NVDA activity was conditional on existing long-spread inventory, portfolio beta, PNR cushion, annualized return on buying power, and discretionary strike selection; he also personally used long-first entries. This design can evaluate a mechanized short-first component, but cannot support claims about the performance or safety of his actual protected book. |

### Assumptions Audit

| Assumption | Classification | Why It Matters |
|------------|----------------|----------------|
| Closing-surface data are available before a 15:55 entry | Contradicted | Creates direct look-ahead bias in every simulated trade. |
| A 10-day fixed-5Δ surface represents the chosen listed option | Fragile | Constant-maturity interpolation, actual expiry, and selected strike can have different skew and term structure. |
| Fixed-delta wing crush tracks the sold contract’s P&L | Unsupported | The fixed-delta strike changes through time, while the original contract’s delta and spot exposure do not disappear. |
| Put and call opportunities can use symmetric trigger thresholds | Contradicted | Pandar treated downside protection and opportunistic call-tail sales differently. |
| High values in both wings indicate that both sides should be sold | Unsupported | A post-shock smile or event repricing can make one side unattractive despite both percentile tests passing. |
| Approximately 4Δ reproduces Pandar’s strike selection | Contradicted | He explicitly emphasized plausible black-swan levels and a range of strikes rather than delta. |
| Nearest 5–12 DTE reproduces his expiry book | Contradicted | His examples included several weeklies through the next monthly cycle and expiries under roughly 26 days. |
| One strike nearer produces a $5-wide spread | Contradicted | Listed spacing varies; “one notch” and “$5-wide” are not interchangeable. |
| A fixed $0.10 retained credit works for nickel option sales | Operationally false | It creates zero or negative buy limits. |
| Close-ask fills are a conservative but decision-safe approximation | Fragile | Missing a fill changes the position from defined-risk spread to naked short, not merely its execution price. |
| May–August 2026 fill bias generalizes to 2023–2026 | Unsupported | Market regime, NVDA price, liquidity, expiries, and option tick behavior changed materially. |
| A one-minute ask touch guarantees execution | Fragile | Quote duration, displayed size, timestamp aggregation, and order state are unspecified. |
| Wing-crush is an adequate threshold-fitting objective | Unsupported | The business outcome is executable P&L and tail-adjusted portfolio utility. |
| The 2023–2026 period is untouched out-of-sample data | Contradicted | The thresholds, regimes, and decision rules were documented after observing the period. |
| A 60% five-session fill rate separates viable from non-viable | Made-up constant | No expected-value, risk, or Pandar evidence supports the cutoff. |
| “NVDA doubles” bounds short-call risk | False | Call losses continue increasing without limit above that scenario. |
| Ten-percent NLV risk per trade controls book-level risk | False | Concurrent positions and correlated shocks can multiply exposure beyond the intended cap. |
| Positive combined P&L is enough to retain or promote the strategy | False | It ignores capital efficiency, uncertainty, side-specific failure, drawdown, and ruin probability. |
| Per-contract P&L is comparable across the 2024 split | Ambiguous | The proposed normalization preserves old-contract dollars but not a consistent post-split risk unit. |
| Holding an unfilled short through expiry is always operationally feasible | Unsupported | Margin escalation, assignment, gaps, and broker liquidation can prevent the modeled hold. |
| Excluding expiries containing earnings removes event risk | Partly supported | It removes scheduled earnings but not adjacent repricing, unscheduled news, or event contamination in fitted surface signals. |
| P1 can be evaluated independently of Pandar’s hedge inventory | Fragile | Existing long spreads and portfolio exposure affected both his willingness to sell and his ability to survive adverse moves. |
