## Codex Strategy Review — Generic Logic
**Verdict**: FAIL (12 findings)

### Findings

| # | Finding | Severity | Category | Details |
|---|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| 1 | The supplied evidence cannot establish fidelity to Pandar’s NVDA book | HIGH | Strategy fidelity | The supplied white paper concerns SPX, 20–40 DTE, roughly one-standard-deviation strikes, minute-scale legging, and prompt closure of failed placements. It does not document this NVDA, 5–12 DTE, approximately 4-delta, multiday naked-short process. The cited PMTraders reconstruction is not substantiated by the supplied context, so results can support only this mechanized variant—not conclusions about what Pandar actually did. |
| 2 | Signal and entry timing create look-ahead bias | CRITICAL | Backtest validity | The trigger uses closing ORATS values but assumes entry at 15:55 on the same session. Those closing values were not then observable. ThetaData EOD quotes likewise are not necessarily the 15:55 executable NBBO. The signal must use timestamped pre-entry data or trade no earlier than the next session. |
| 3 | The trigger is disconnected from the option being traded | HIGH | Trigger logic | A constant-maturity 10-day, fixed-5-delta surface point can behave differently from the selected listed expiry and 2–6-delta contract. Fixed-delta series also migrate across strikes as spot moves. Consequently, measured “wing crush” need not produce a gain or buyback fill in the original option. |
| 4 | Threshold fitting optimizes the wrong objective and invites overfitting | HIGH | Optimization | Maximizing five-session surface wing-crush does not maximize executable P&L, buyback probability, or tail-adjusted return; spot movement can overwhelm IV compression. The 85/70 baseline, 12-signals-per-year floor, candidate-search space, and flat-maximum rule lack derivation. Clustered daily signals are treated as independent observations, and the test-window sensitivity grid adds selection risk. |
| 5 | Instrument selection does not define one reproducible strategy | HIGH | Instrument design | Approximately 4 delta and the 25%-OTM fallback are economically different rules, while the requested EOD dataset does not guarantee the greeks needed to determine which rule applies. “One listed strike nearer” produces variable widths, not consistently $5. Pooling those trades without width or risk normalization confounds instrument choice with data availability and listing conventions. |
| 6 | Buyback and fill mechanics can generate impossible or misleading outcomes | HIGH | Execution | Entry permits a $0.05 bid while baseline retained credit is $0.10, producing a nonpositive buy limit. Orders begin next session despite being described as rested immediately. Filling every qualifying order at its limit makes “median credit kept” tautologically equal to the chosen constant. A close-ask rule misses intraday fills that change the position from naked to defined-risk, not merely its execution price. |
| 7 | The fill-bias validation cannot validate the historical backtest | HIGH | Validation | May–August 2026 cannot establish fill behavior across the distinct 2023–2025 regimes, and a scalar caveat cannot propagate nonlinear changes in fill status and tail exposure. The stated 171 sessions between 2026-05-04 and 2026-08-14 is chronologically impossible. Even one-minute ask touches require assumptions about quote duration, displayed size, queue position, and whether the order was live. |
| 8 | The “free spread” omits its economic opportunity cost | HIGH | Counterfactual | When the nearer option reaches the limit, the farther short should normally cost less to repurchase. Closing that short would therefore retain more cash than buying the nearer option. The difference is the economic cost of keeping the vertical. Reporting its later payoff without comparison to same-time buy-to-close can make an inferior transformation appear free. |
| 9 | Naked-call tail risk invalidates the proposed sizing rule | CRITICAL | Tail risk | Put loss at zero is bounded, but naked-call loss is unbounded; “NVDA doubles” is only one scenario, not notional at risk. A 10%-NLV allowance per trade ignores overlapping expiries, simultaneous sides, overnight gaps, margin expansion, forced liquidation, assignment, and correlated exposure. Excluding sizing and margin survival from pass/fail permits promotion of a book that may be impossible to hold through the modeled loss path. |
| 10 | P&L and fee normalization mix incompatible risk units | HIGH | Accounting | Dividing pre-split premiums by ten while multiplying the contract multiplier by ten preserves one legacy contract’s dollars, but that contract represents ten post-split contracts’ exposure. “Per-contract” results therefore overweight pre-split trades and make regime comparisons and account scaling invalid. Fees must also be defined per executed leg and normalized consistently; cash-intrinsic settlement assumes away physical assignment and funding constraints. |
| 11 | The pass/hold/stop gates do not establish viability | CRITICAL | Decision logic | The 60% fill hurdle, half-median-income loss allowance, three-of-four-regime rule, and sign-flip condition have no economic or statistical basis. “Trailing-12-month median income” is undefined, regimes are unequal and retrospectively chosen, and 2026 intraday coverage cannot determine fill-induced sign changes in earlier regimes. Side-versus-combined application and decision precedence are ambiguous, allowing a catastrophic call side or one dominant loss regime to be hidden. |
| 12 | The design lacks the falsification needed for a promotion decision | HIGH | Falsification | Positive returns could be generic short-volatility carry rather than signal skill. Required controls include matched nonsignal dates, unconditional and randomized entries, same-option buy-to-close, immediate defined-risk verticals, naked-to-expiry, alternative strike rules, known Pandar examples, leave-one-shock-out analysis, block-bootstrap uncertainty, and explicit overnight gap and margin-liquidation stresses. Roughly 3.6 observed years in one preselected stock cannot estimate a 4-delta short’s ruin risk. |

### Assumptions Audit

| Assumption | Classification | Why It Matters |
|------------|----------------|----------------|
| The supplied sources document Pandar’s actual NVDA P1 process | Contradicted | The supplied white paper documents a materially different SPX framework, while the asserted NVDA reconstruction lacks supporting evidence here. |
| Closing-surface values are available before a 15:55 entry | Contradicted | This creates direct look-ahead bias. |
| An EOD bid represents an executable 15:55 bid | Unsupported | Quote timing and execution prices can materially change the thin premiums being modeled. |
| A fixed-5-delta, constant-10-day wing represents the selected contract | Fragile | Expiry, strike, skew, and delta migration can produce different economics. |
| Five-session wing-crush predicts trade P&L | Unsupported | Spot movement, theta, vega, and contract migration are omitted from the fitting objective. |
| Symmetric put and call thresholds are economically justified | Unsupported | The two sides have different skew, drift, gap behavior, and loss bounds. |
| The 85/70 thresholds identify genuine richness | Made-up constants | No economic calibration, statistical uncertainty, or prospective commitment supports them. |
| Twelve signals per year is an appropriate optimization constraint | Made-up constant | It controls sample count without establishing independence, capacity, or decision usefulness. |
| Approximately 4 delta and 25% OTM are interchangeable | False | Their relationship varies sharply with DTE, volatility, skew, and spot regime. |
| One listed strike nearer creates a comparable $5-wide position | False | Strike spacing varies and produces different maximum payoff and risk. |
| Every eligible sale supports a positive $0.10 buy limit | Operationally false | Sales at or below $0.10 yield zero or negative limits. |
| Starting the order next session reproduces an immediate resting order | Contradicted | Same-session reversals and fills are excluded. |
| Median retained credit is an informative result | Tautological | Under the modeled fill rule it is fixed mechanically at the selected constant. |
| Close-ask fills are conservative but decision-equivalent | False | A missed fill changes the position’s risk class from a spread to a naked short. |
| May–August 2026 fill behavior generalizes to all regimes | Unsupported | Liquidity, price level, volatility, and gap behavior changed across the test window. |
| A one-minute ask touch guarantees execution | Fragile | Size, duration, queue position, and order-state assumptions remain unspecified. |
| The resulting vertical is economically free | False | Retaining it sacrifices the larger cash profit available from repurchasing the cheaper far option. |
| “NVDA doubles” bounds short-call exposure | False | Call losses remain unbounded above that scenario. |
| A 10%-NLV limit per trade controls total book risk | False | Concurrent positions and common shocks can aggregate far beyond that limit. |
| An unfilled short can always be held to expiry | Unsupported | Margin calls, broker liquidation, assignment, and funding constraints can force an earlier exit. |
| Per-contract P&L is comparable across the split | False | A normalized legacy contract carries ten times a post-split contract’s exposure. |
| The 2023–2026 window is epistemically out of sample | Fragile | The specification, regimes, shocks, and decision gates were written after observing the full period. |
| A 60% five-session fill rate separates viable from nonviable strategies | Made-up constant | It is not tied to expected value, capital usage, or acceptable tail loss. |
| Recent fill validation can determine sign changes in earlier regimes | False | No intraday observations exist for those regimes. |
| Combined positive P&L is sufficient evidence of viability | False | It can conceal side-specific ruin risk, poor capital efficiency, and statistically insignificant carry. |
| Historical NVDA observations adequately measure naked-short tail risk | Unsupported | Rare gaps and unbounded call losses cannot be estimated reliably from this short, single-name sample. |
