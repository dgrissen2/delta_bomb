## Codex Strategy Review — Generic Logic
**Verdict**: FAIL (9 findings)

### Findings

| # | Finding | Severity | Category | Details |
|---|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| 1 | “Buys speed” is unsupported and contradicted by the available baseline | HIGH | Conclusion validity | The baseline output contains no time-to-fill measure, so the reported trigger median of 5 minutes has no documented comparator. Recomputing the anytime baseline from the same SPX OHLC with 60 future bars gives a conditional median of 5 minutes—identical to the trigger. Establishing speed requires a day-clustered survival or cumulative-hit comparison, including non-completions; the current evidence does not establish it. |
| 2 | The trigger-versus-anytime comparison is neither mechanically equivalent nor causal | HIGH | Experimental design | `spx_legin_ev.py` evaluates 60 future bars, while `spx_touch_stats.py` slices `i0+1:i0+N`, providing only 59 future bars. More fundamentally, uniform clock starts are not the counterfactual to waiting for a stall/reclaim: they include unrelated states and some trigger periods. The relevant comparison is entry when the setup arms versus waiting for its trigger, matched within day, time, volatility, and setup. |
| 3 | The reported results do not reproduce from the supplied canonical output | HIGH | Evidence integrity | The conclusion reports 8,353 fires, about 10 per day, and a 21.1% low-break rate; the supplied script output and parquet contain 7,337 fires, 8.68 per day, and 23.7%. The 40+ point bucket is reported as 0.84 completion versus 0.857 in the output. Section 2d reports 12,676 starts versus 12,675 stored. Until the version and parameters are reconciled, the tables are not auditable. |
| 4 | The preregistered validation was not performed | HIGH | Statistical validation | The prior specification required walk-forward or held-out results, day-clustered 90% intervals, and option-level economics. The conclusion instead pools 2022–2026, supplies no intervals, and uses heavily overlapping 60/120-minute outcomes every 15 minutes. A single pullback can also refire repeatedly without rearming. Thousands of rows therefore do not represent thousands of independent trials. Multiple feature windows, horizons, outcomes, and quartile views further increase false-discovery risk. |
| 5 | “ER/flips carry nothing” overstates and partly contradicts the tables | HIGH | Inference | Across ER quartiles, completion rises 2.6 pp, both-direction completion rises 5.7 pp, and round trips rise 5.4 pp. In the stored ER-by-range cross-tab, the middle-range tercile changes from about 82.5% to 77.5% completion and 60.5% to 54.6% both-direction completion across ER terciles—not “≤2 pp everywhere.” Direction-flip quartiles also show about a 4.2 pp monotonic change in both-direction completion. These may ultimately be too small or unstable to trade, but that requires a prespecified materiality test with clustered intervals. VWAP flips and alligator state were not measured at all, so they cannot be “dropped for good” from this test. |
| 6 | Fixed +4 bp spot movement is confounded with volatility and is not SPX-put completion | HIGH | Proxy validity | In a high-range regime, a fixed spot threshold is mechanically easier to touch. The actual move required to fill the second SPX put depends on delta, gamma, vega, skew, DTE, bid/ask width, and limit price; those variables also change with volatility. Therefore the range result principally demonstrates volatility persistence under a fixed barrier, not higher bomb plant probability. The acknowledged option-level test is required before operational use. |
| 7 | “Sizing dial, not selection signal” does not follow from the reported outcomes | HIGH | Decision logic | High range raises completion by about 13.4 pp but raises adverse-over-20-bp frequency by 7.8 pp and adverse-over-40-bp frequency by 4.5 pp. The worsening fills-per-tail ratio could justify avoiding high-range states, accepting them at reduced size, or preferring them—depending on payoff and utility. Binary exceedances omit tail magnitude, expected short-leg loss, stop slippage, margin use, and incomplete-leg cost. Consequently “half size,” “tight clock,” and allowing quiet-market legs to rest longer are invented policies, not results of these tables. Falsification requires option-level net EV and ES₉₅ by range bucket, both raw and volatility-normalized. |
| 8 | Time-of-day and regime confounding remain unresolved | MEDIUM | Confounding | High prior-hour ranges are disproportionately observed early: 10:30 supplies about 13.9% of high-range observations but only 1.6% of quiet-range observations, while baseline completion itself declines through the day. Trigger fires also have a different intraday distribution from uniform clock starts. The range ordering appears descriptively strong, but its magnitude and thresholds require time-stratified, day-clustered analysis with year, event-day, and volatility-regime controls. |
| 9 | The appended trend-based leg-order rule is outside the evidence tested | MEDIUM | Scope creep | The stall and range tables do not test whether an UP state should imply sell-first, DOWN should imply long-first, or CHOP should imply long-first. That operational addition needs SPX-index-put outcomes for each leg order, including unpaired-leg P&L and tail loss. It should remain a hypothesis rather than be added to v2 from these conclusions. |

### Assumptions Audit

| Assumption | Classification | Why It Matters |
|------------|----------------|----------------|
| Stall/reclaim reduces time-to-fill versus anytime entry | Contradicted | The supplied baseline omitted this metric; a like-for-like reconstruction gives the same 5-minute median. |
| Uniform clock starts represent the counterfactual to waiting for a trigger | Invalid | They do not condition on the setup being armed and therefore mix selection, timing, and market-state effects. |
| The trigger does not improve completion probability | Overstated | It fails the preregistered +5 pp materiality bar, but the observed increase is roughly 1 pp after correcting the baseline window; “no material improvement” is supportable, “no improvement” is not. |
| Thousands of observations provide independent statistical evidence | Rejected | Starts, trigger refires, and outcome windows overlap heavily within the same 845 sessions. |
| A +4 bp SPX move represents a second-leg fill across 20–40 DTE SPX puts | Unvalidated proxy | Option sensitivity and executable prices vary with volatility, skew, maturity, and liquidity. |
| ER and direction flips are materially null after controlling for range | Contradicted or unresolved | Stored cross-tabs contain effects exceeding the claimed 2 pp ceiling, and no clustered uncertainty or holdout test is supplied. |
| VWAP flips and alligator state were tested by section 2d | Rejected | Neither variable exists in the supplied implementation or output. |
| Prior-hour range adds information beyond time of day and contemporaneous volatility | Partially supported | The ordering is strong but remains a fixed-barrier volatility result without adjusted or option-level validation. |
| Tail-event frequency is sufficient to determine sizing versus selection | Unsupported | Expected utility depends on tail severity, fill economics, stop execution, capital use, and the overlap between fills and adverse events. |
| A 30 bp cutoff and half-size rule are calibrated risk controls | Invented | No optimization, loss target, or out-of-sample comparison derives either constant. |
| Allowing a leg to rest longer in quiet markets is safer | Unsupported | Lower short-horizon tail frequency does not establish lower total exposure risk when the holding window is lengthened. |
| The reported tables and parquet refer to the same run | Contradicted | Counts and several summary statistics materially disagree. |
