## Codex Strategy Review — Generic Logic
**Verdict**: FAIL (10 findings)

### Findings

| # | Finding | Severity | Category | Details |
|---|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| 1 | Supplied evidence rejects positive expectancy | CRITICAL | Core conclusion | Every tested variant has negative mean daily P&L: anchor −3.23, long-first −2.02, ladder3 −6.39, and short3 −6.21 points. Short3’s +3.25 median is overwhelmed by a −167.5-point tail. Calling the resulting spreads “costless” ignores the much larger P&L generated while legs are unpaired. |
| 2 | The ATM anchor is uneconomic for the tested one-bomb workflow | HIGH | Anchor economics | The anchor loses 1.35 points per day while producing 1.06 bombs, or approximately 1.27 points/$127 per bomb. Mean entry credit is only 0.50 points/$50 per bomb, so anchor drag is 2.55 times the planting credit. It consumes 88% of the paper’s conditional 1.45-point median exit value when entry credit is zero. The anchor variant also underperforms long-first by 1.21 points/day and has a worse observed minimum, −52 versus −20. |
| 3 | The bomb’s expected value cannot be inferred from the paper’s 1.45 median | HIGH | Statistical inference | The 1.45 figure is a median closing value for 57 spreads selected for individual closure, not an unconditional mean across all attempts. It does not incorporate failed plants, abandoned legs, anchors, spreads held as hedges, commissions, or selection rules. Consequently, anchor cost can be quantified, but bomb expected value cannot. |
| 4 | Sell-first substitutes catastrophic short-put exposure for a $500 spread payoff | CRITICAL | Tail risk | Until the long fills, selling the 7520 put exposes one contract to roughly \((7520-41)\times100=\$747,900\) of theoretical expiration loss. That is about 51% of stated NLV. SPX is cash-settled with a $100 multiplier, according to [Cboe’s specifications](https://www.cboe.com/tradable-products/sp-500/spx-options/). Buying power indicates broker capacity, not tolerable loss. The −167.5-point short3 strategy-day already demonstrates severe negative skew, while 102 sessions cannot characterize crash risk. |
| 5 | No proposed sizing or stop rule actually makes the naked interval safe | HIGH | Risk controls | A bullish opinion does not bound loss. The plan lacks a dollar loss budget, maximum concurrent shorts, maximum unpaired time, underlying or option-price trigger, event exclusions, and forced-conversion rule. The resting long limit moves farther from execution during the adverse falling-market path. Stops can also gap or slip; only an already-filled protective long produces defined risk. |
| 6 | The requested “next two orders” are unsupported and operationally ambiguous | HIGH | Execution logic | The existing +7525/−7520 is complete and needs no repair. If scaling the identical spread, the contract sequence would be another 7520 STO followed—only after its actual fill—by a 7525 BTO. Selling the 7525 instead would offset the existing 7525 long; a subsequent 7530 purchase would leave an economic +7530/−7520 ten-wide spread, not two independent five-wides. With a 40.7 short sale, the proposed 40.6 long limit is 1.10 points below the current 41.7 ask and has no adverse no-fill branch. Given negative backtested means and absent controls, no additional unpaired order is supported “right now.” |
| 7 | Backtest P&L columns do not reconcile | HIGH | Accounting integrity | Anchor credit of +0.53 and anchor P&L of −1.35 imply −0.82 points, not reported total P&L of −3.23; 2.41 points are unexplained. Long-first gives \(0.46-3.14=-2.68\), not −2.02. Ladder3 gives \(1.45-10.11=-8.66\), not −6.39. Short3 reports +1.61 credit and zero leftover-long P&L but −6.21 total. Missing components prevent attribution of losses or independent verification. |
| 8 | Five-minute data cannot validate the claimed execution edge without stronger controls | HIGH | Backtest validity | Median seven-minute planting and exact limit fills require timestamped executable bid/ask data, sequencing within each bar, latency, queue assumptions, slippage, commissions, strike-selection rules, and treatment of simultaneous stop and target touches. None are supplied. A 102-session 2024–2025 sample is also inadequate for a short-convexity tail decision. |
| 9 | Probability of touch is not an economic edge | MEDIUM | Causal logic | “Probability of touch is roughly twice delta” does not imply positive expectancy: option prices already reflect path probability, volatility, and skew. Touching a strike also does not guarantee the second-leg limit fills profitably. The paper supplies no comparison against immediately buying the vertical, delta-hedged legging, or a matched random-entry control. |
| 10 | The 20Δ and 90-DTE alternatives cannot be ranked per unit risk | HIGH | Alternatives | The 20Δ backtest has negative mean P&L, while the 90-to-60-DTE method is supported only by Discord anecdotes from traders who already wanted long puts. The alternatives differ in delta, premium at risk, holding time, completion probability, and tail shape. No common denominator such as expected shortfall, maximum unpaired loss, portfolio hedge benefit, or capital-time exposure is provided. A portfolio-level ATM anchor could still be rational if its crash covariance is explicitly valued, but that case is not tested here. |

### Assumptions Audit

| Assumption | Classification | Why It Matters |
|------------|----------------|----------------|
| A completed credit spread makes the overall process costless | False | Completed-spread basis omits losses and exposure incurred while legging. |
| The 1.45 median is the bomb’s expected value | Invalid inference | It is a conditional median from a selected subset, not an unconditional mean. |
| A 95% planting rate establishes profitability | Unsupported | Five-percent failures can dominate many small credits; failure severity is missing. |
| A bullish intraday view justifies selling first | Unvalidated | No forecasting accuracy, calibration, or conditional backtest is supplied. |
| One contract is safe because the account has $1.2M buying power | False proxy | Broker margin capacity is not a risk budget and can change sharply under stress. |
| A lower resting buy order controls short-put risk | False | It becomes less likely to fill precisely when the short put is losing. |
| The ATM anchor’s cost can be amortized across several bombs | Refuted in tested workflow | The anchor variant produces only 1.06 bombs/day on average. |
| The anchor should always be evaluated on standalone P&L | Incomplete | A genuine hedge must be assessed through total-portfolio drawdown and expected-shortfall reduction. |
| Five-minute bars reproduce executable seven-minute legging | Unsupported | Intrabar path, quotes, spreads, queue position, and latency can reverse results. |
| The 2024–2025 sample represents SPX crash tails | Fragile | It is too short to estimate rare gap, volatility, and liquidity regimes. |
| Discord outcomes establish the superiority of long-DTE planting | Anecdotal | The observations are self-selected and lack failures, denominator, and risk-adjusted comparison. |
| Adjacent bombs remain independent positions | Operationally false | Shared strikes net economically and may transform several nominal five-wide spreads into one wider spread. |
