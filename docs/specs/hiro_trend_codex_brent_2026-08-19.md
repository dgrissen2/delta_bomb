## Codex Strategy Review — Generic Logic
**Verdict**: FAIL (10 findings)

### Findings

| # | Finding | Severity | Category | Details |
|---|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| 1 | The experiment does not measure the actual trade objective | HIGH | Construct validity | A 3–7 point SPX continuation is only a proxy for completing the spread at zero/credit. Completion depends on both option-leg prices, strike/delta selection, IV and skew changes, bid/ask spreads, latency, and commissions. The primary outcome must replay executable quotes and determine whether the second leg could actually fill at the target net debit. |
| 2 | The unpaired-short risk test does not correspond to the stated stop | HIGH | Risk mechanics | “Adverse >15 SPX points” does not test the rule “buy back at sale +3.5,” because option-price movement is state-dependent and nonlinear. Gaps can also bypass that premium stop. The experiment must calculate leg-level mark-to-market loss, stop execution with slippage, maximum loss before exit, and gap scenarios. |
| 3 | Signal and entry timing are not sufficiently defined to exclude leakage | HIGH | Experimental integrity | The document does not establish whether a minute-start decision uses only HIRO observations published before that timestamp, how 5-second observations are resampled, or whether the minute’s SPX high/low participates in entry qualification. Every feature, pullback trigger, leg fill, and outcome needs an explicit timestamp and executable-price convention. |
| 4 | Minute observations are treated as if they were independent opportunities | HIGH | Statistics | The 33 UP observations and seven qualifying DOWN entries come from only three days per side and likely contain repeated minutes from the same trend episode. Overlapping 15/30/60-minute outcome windows create severe dependence. The experimental unit should be a non-overlapping trend episode or session, with one pre-specified entry and session-level inference. |
| 5 | The sweep is an in-sample multiple-testing exercise | HIGH | Overfitting | At least 1,788 UP cells with \(n\ge30\) were searched, making the best observed +0.061 lift unsurprising and unusable as evidence. The primary specification must now be frozen without reference to the winning cells; future sessions must be evaluated out of sample, with sweep results explicitly exploratory. |
| 6 | The first-run primary result is adverse to the thesis | HIGH | Evidence | The primary UP cell materially underperformed its baseline at every reported target: for +5 points, 0.030 vs 0.145 at 15 minutes, 0.061 vs 0.418 at 30, and 0.152 vs 0.509 at 60. DOWN has only seven entries and is mixed rather than confirmatory. The current evidence therefore rejects deploying the specified primary rule; it does not justify threshold refinement. |
| 7 | “Smooth HIRO trend” can be a mathematical artifact of accumulated flow | MEDIUM | Measurement | Regressing a cumulative series on time mechanically raises persistence and \(R^2\), even when increments have little forecasting value. EMA slope, sign consistency, cumulative-line \(R^2\), and drawdown are also correlated transformations of the same data. Smoothness should be benchmarked against shuffled or block-bootstrapped flow increments and tested for incremental prediction beyond contemporaneous price. |
| 8 | The proposed confirmations are not independent | MEDIUM | Signal logic | “All” contains next-expiry flow, while call/put components contribute to total flow. Agreement among these overlapping series can be algebraic rather than independent confirmation. Each scope needs an exact inclusion formula, sign convention, and overlap-aware ablation showing incremental value. |
| 9 | Mechanics and controls do not identify HIRO’s incremental contribution | HIGH | Causal logic | All five sessions were positive-gamma sessions, where observed HIRO may reflect price-following flow or absorption rather than forward hedging pressure. Price confirmation further risks making HIRO a redundant label for an already-established price trend. The test needs matched price-only controls, HIRO-only and shuffled-HIRO controls, gamma-regime interaction terms, and a pre-specified comparison of incremental lift. |
| 10 | No valid pass/fail rule exists for 5–20 sessions | HIGH | Decision design | Thresholds such as consistency 0.70, \(R^2=0.80\), drawdown 0.25, and magnitude 0.5 are not justified or fully unit-defined, and no minimum session count, effect size, confidence interval, tail-loss bound, or superiority margin is registered. Five sessions cannot establish edge, regime robustness, execution quality, or short-leg tail safety. A defensible gate would require out-of-sample session-level improvement over price-only control, actual net-fill improvement, and no material worsening of loss quantiles across substantially more independent sessions. |

### Assumptions Audit

| Assumption | Classification | Why It Matters |
|------------|----------------|----------------|
| A smooth HIRO line represents continuing dealer hedging pressure rather than accumulated noise or price-following flow | Unsupported | Without lead-lag and shuffled-flow tests, the signal may describe the past rather than forecast the required continuation. |
| A 3–7 point SPX move implies the spread can be completed for zero/credit | Invalid as stated | Option prices, volatility, skew, spreads, and execution determine the achievable net price. |
| Minute-start features contain no information from the minute being traded | Unverified | Timestamp or resampling leakage could manufacture apparent predictiveness. |
| Each qualifying minute is a separate trade opportunity | Invalid | Clustered minutes and overlapping horizons greatly overstate effective sample size. |
| Call/put and all/next-expiry agreement provide independent confirmation | Fragile | These series overlap by construction and can repeat the same underlying information. |
| Positive HIRO should predict upward SPX continuation, and negative HIRO downward continuation | Unsupported | The mapping depends on whether flow is causal, reactive, absorbed, or amplified by the prevailing gamma regime. |
| The selected smoothness thresholds have structural meaning | Data-dependent | Their units and rationale are absent, while the large sweep creates selection bias. |
| The same-minute baseline is an adequate counterfactual | Unverified | Matching variables, sampling rules, episode dependence, and comparability across reported controls are unspecified. |
| A premium stop at sale +3.5 can be represented by a 15-point SPX adverse threshold | Invalid | Option delta, gamma, vega, liquidity, and gaps break any fixed conversion. |
| Five positive-gamma sessions can support a trading decision | Unsupported | They cannot estimate stable edge, regime interactions, day-level variance, or tail loss. |
| The best sweep cells indicate a promising rule | Invalid | Selecting maxima across thousands of in-sample variants produces optimistic results even under no true edge. |
| Price confirmation improves HIRO rather than merely selecting an existing price trend | Unproven | The stronger price-only result suggests HIRO may add no incremental information and may reduce fill probability. |
