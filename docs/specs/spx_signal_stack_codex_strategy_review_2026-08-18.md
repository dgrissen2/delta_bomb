## Codex Strategy Review — Generic Logic
**Verdict**: FAIL (13 findings)

### Findings

| # | Finding | Severity | Category | Details |
|---|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| 1 | The proposed stack has not been tested as specified | HIGH | Validation | The document admits the combination is unmeasured, yet presents it operationally. The proposed `spx_legin_ev.py` test covers only stall plus reclaim, not the regime veto, setup gates, full 2-of-3 trigger, IV veto, execution rules, anti-signals, or alternate leg orders. Success of that reduced rule would not validate the deployed stack. |
| 2 | Evidence is transferred across incompatible instruments, regimes, and horizons | HIGH | External validity | Sources mix SPY OHLCV, parity-derived SPX spot, real SPX OHLC, 0DTE short calls, and the target 20–40 DTE SPX put spread. The alligator sample is 107 SPY days with `open<VT` and negative gamma; rollover work concerns positive-gamma 0DTE calls. ATR normalization does not establish that direction, threshold, or option-price behavior survives these transfers. |
| 3 | The setup amplitude rule is mathematically inconsistent | HIGH | Units and thresholds | Since `u=max(0.10×IM,4)`, `2u=max(0.20×IM,8)`. Requiring a run of “at least 2u or at least 0.2IM” collapses to the weaker `0.2IM` test and can admit less than eight points when the floor binds. The proposed 2u arm, 3u trend boundary, and claim that the option leg needs about 0.75u were not measured for these 20–40 DTE puts. |
| 4 | Failure of the alligator continuation strategy does not prove a fade signal | HIGH | Invalid inference | `strong_up()` produced late, poor continuation entries; it never tested fading them. The document converts “if anything, fade” into an exhaustion detector, mirrors it to down moves, and applies it to sell-put timing. It also omits source conditions such as VWAP position, bar color, EMA9/20 slopes, and range-CV consistency. This is a new detector, not the cited one. |
| 5 | The chop gate contradicts the supplied results | HIGH | Evidence contradiction | `ctx_chop_brief.md` reports ER correlation near zero and says six-plus VWAP/color flips produced the best mean result; it explicitly warns against blanket chop filtering. The stack nevertheless treats ER below 0.3 or four flips as confirmation that both legs will fill, while inventing a rolling 60-bar ER-above-0.5 veto. Neither fill completion nor these cut-points was tested. |
| 6 | Stall and reclaim do not match their cited implementations | HIGH | Code fidelity | `exit_ml(L)` tests whether current spot is below spot L bars ago; it is not “no new high for L bars.” `stall_exit()` uses two declining short-call closes, not SPX swing structure. The toolkit requires no new session high plus VWAP loss. Likewise, the source TPM is a running mean, not a five-bar mean, and the cited ride exit uses two closes below EMA9 rather than a one-close EMA5 reclaim. The failed-lower-low rule is not the exact mirror of the source’s lower-swing-high condition. |
| 7 | The 2-of-3 trigger double-counts correlated price evidence | HIGH | Trigger design | Stall, reclaim, and reversal are overlapping descriptions of the same few bars, not independent confirmations. A single strong close can simultaneously create a reclaim and reversal bar after a mechanically satisfied stall. Alternatives within each vote, the 3-versus-5-bar choice, session-versus-swing extreme, vote expiry, and required coincidence window are unspecified, creating substantial researcher and operator discretion. |
| 8 | Causal timing and executable fills are not defined | HIGH | Look-ahead and execution | “Last swing extreme” can contain pivot look-ahead unless defined causally. One- and five-minute triggers require the completed bar’s close, high, low, and ATR, so the earliest defensible execution is the next quote—not the same bar close. The brutal-reversal code exits at the triggering bar’s close, while the proposed strategy assumes an immediate bid fill. Resting-order tests also require quote sequence, bid/ask, and queue-aware fill logic rather than bar-touch inference. |
| 9 | The IV veto misstates both timeframe and source definition | HIGH | Timeframe mismatch | The correlations were calculated at 10:30 on 104 0DTE VT-loss days against subsequent recovery. They do not validate a rolling one-minute veto for 20–40 DTE puts throughout the session. The corrected source defines the ATM gate as IV above its 09:35 value—not “still rising”; many selected observations had already experienced IV crush. Put-skew has no operational threshold and came from a multiple-tested descriptive analysis. |
| 10 | Time-window and failed-probe rules are asymmetric transfers | MEDIUM | Generalization | The time-of-day and poke-depth evidence comes from five-minute 0DTE touches of a level above spot. It is transferred to downside VT, Put Wall, and round-strike probes without a dip-side test. Round strikes are not supported by the supplied source, and the 0.10–0.25 IM probe band is broad enough to label ordinary proximity as a failed level test. |
| 11 | The naked-short cap mixes units and does not reliably bound loss | HIGH | Tail risk | `sale+3.5` is an option-premium threshold; equating it to about 15 SPX points assumes stable delta and ignores gamma, vega, skew, spreads, and gaps. A $350 planned loss already exceeds twice the cited $145 median bomb exit, with no estimated stop frequency. Buying the next-lower put converts the position into a bullish credit spread rather than the intended long-higher-strike bomb, and no management branch is supplied for that new exposure. |
| 12 | The acceptance metrics do not measure the strategy’s economic objective | HIGH | Objective mismatch | Every supplied legging variant has negative mean day P&L, with unpaired-leg losses dominating credits. Improving 60-minute one-way-move probability by five points—or reducing a spot-MAE threshold by five points—does not demonstrate executable net credit, plant rate, expected P&L, or expected shortfall. The trigger could improve spot timing while reducing completion probability or worsening option-price fills. |
| 13 | The proposed holdout is already contaminated | HIGH | Research design | Components and cut-points were selected using studies extending through 2025–26, including explicit 2024–26 comparisons. Those years cannot then serve as an untouched holdout. Numerous alternative thresholds and OR branches further increase selection bias. Validation requires a frozen specification with nested or walk-forward selection, followed by genuinely unseen or prospective data and day-clustered uncertainty intervals. |

### Assumptions Audit

| Assumption | Classification | Why It Matters |
|------------|----------------|----------------|
| Individually observed components will add value when combined | Unsupported | Correlated filters can merely reduce sample size or amplify selection bias; only an end-to-end test can establish incremental value. |
| A failed trend-following alligator is a profitable fade signal | Invalid inference | Failure of continuation establishes neither reversal probability nor option-leg profitability. |
| SPY one-minute thresholds transfer to SPX one-minute bars | Unsupported | The instruments have different raw scales, microstructure, price formation, and volume availability. |
| 0DTE call-timing evidence transfers to 20–40 DTE puts | Unsupported | Delta, gamma, theta, vega, skew, and quote response differ materially. |
| Top-detection rules are symmetric bottom-detection rules | Unsupported | Down moves and volatility expansion are structurally asymmetric; mirroring signs is not validation. |
| Low ER or frequent flips predicts successful two-leg completion | Contradicted | The supplied chop study found near-zero predictive correlation and favorable results in its highest-flip group. |
| The three trigger votes provide independent confirmation | Likely false | They reuse the same prices and often fire from the same closing bar. |
| `2u or 0.2IM` expresses the intended minimum run | Internally inconsistent | The OR operator weakens the 2u floor whenever the four-point minimum determines `u`. |
| A completed-bar trigger can be filled at that bar’s close or bid | Operationally unrealistic | Detection and execution must be sequenced using the next available quote. |
| A 3.5-point option stop is equivalent to roughly 15 SPX points | Fragile and unsupported | The mapping changes with Greeks, IV, skew, time, and market liquidity. |
| A 60-minute clock sufficiently controls naked-short tail risk | Unsupported | Time limits do not protect against discontinuous moves or failed/slipped exits. |
| No SpotGamma levels implies long-first is economically superior | Unsupported | Long-first reduces naked-short exposure but the supplied backtest shows material leftover-long losses. |
| A five-percentage-point spot-path improvement makes bombs profitable | Unsupported | Profit depends on bid/ask execution, completion, credit, leftover-leg loss, and terminal spread value. |
| 2025–26 constitutes clean out-of-sample evidence | False | Those years informed component selection and threshold interpretation before the proposed test. |
