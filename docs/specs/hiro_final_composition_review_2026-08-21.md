1. **Does the production answer follow?** Mostly, but the evidence supports a more conservative composition:

- Use the TAPE rule as the primary setup whenever its high-range/negative-flow conditions exist.
- Use BASE as the general opportunity generator, but treat it as provisional: its newest-session fill rate was below baseline.
- Deduplicate overlapping signals, permit only one live leg at a time, and cap the day at three entries.
- Retain HIRO only for precisely defined vetoes and flow-shutoff exits. Clarify the veto wording before launch—“no long” and “no sell-first” during strongly negative flow cannot both apply without additional state definitions.
- Do not yet claim 1–2 completed bombs/day as an expectation. The observed inputs suggest that as an optimistic target, particularly because BASE and TAPE opportunities may overlap.

2. **Biggest remaining risk:** multiplicity combined with regime concentration. Many rules and thresholds were examined over only eight highly related August sessions; matched controls repair individual comparisons but do not eliminate selection bias across the full research process. The down-day TAPE result may also be dominated by a particular high-volatility/down-week regime. BASE’s deterioration on the newest three sessions is the clearest live warning. The partial August 21 session is secondary: report results both including and excluding it, but it is unlikely to be the central problem.

3. **Pre-registered 10-session acceptance test**

Freeze all definitions, thresholds, precedence rules, vetoes, scratches, and tie-breaking before session one. No discretionary overrides or parameter changes. Log every qualifying signal, including skipped signals and the exact reason.

Primary operational criteria:

- Signals on at least 7/10 sessions.
- One to three executable entries on at least 6/10 sessions.
- At least eight completed bombs total and at least one completion on 6/10 sessions.
- No more than three entries per session; one leg live at a time.

Efficacy criteria:

- BASE: at least 20 qualifying signals, fill rate ≥0.45 and no worse than its frozen clock-matched control.
- TAPE: at least eight qualifying episodes, fill rate ≥0.70 and at least 10 percentage points above the frozen midpoint-matched control.
- Report BASE, TAPE, and composite results separately; overlapping signals count once in the composite.

Risk criteria:

- Adverse excursion >10 points on ≤10% of entries and no more than one such trade.
- Median realized loss on scratches ≤3 points.
- No more than one scratch that would subsequently have completed the original target within the fixed 60-minute horizon.
- Results must still satisfy the safety criteria after excluding the best session.

If branch sample minima are not reached, label that branch **inconclusive**, not failed or validated. Any rule change resets the test.

**Verdict:** Ship it as a frozen paper-trading experiment, not yet as a validated production playbook. The TAPE branch is the strongest entry rule; HIRO’s defensible contribution is risk control; BASE earns continued testing because it supplies frequency, but its prospective stability is the central question. The next ten sessions should test the composition exactly once, with no further search disguised as refinement.
