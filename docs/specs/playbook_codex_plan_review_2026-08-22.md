## Codex Plan Review — Verdict: FAIL (14 findings)

| # | Finding | Severity | Dimension | Details |
|---|---------|----------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | Late-day rules are mutually incompatible | HIGH | Internal consistency | The playbook permits entries through 14:30 and gives them a 60-minute clock, forbids anything unpaired after 14:30, yet schedules incomplete-leg resolution at 15:30. Any entry after 13:30 can violate one of these rules. |
| 2 | Leg-order rules have no total precedence | HIGH | Logic | Section 3 can require long-first while BASE mandates sell-first, or sell-first while TAPE mandates long-first. It never states whether branch instructions override trend state, Call-Wall positioning, or the below-VT safety veto. |
| 3 | The acceptance test omits mandatory pass criteria | HIGH | Test adequacy | Compared with evidence §7, the status board omits at least one completion on 6 of 10 days, BASE ≥20 signals, TAPE ≥8 episodes, BASE not below its control, at most one scratch that later would have completed, and safety results excluding the best session. The shortened test can pass a composition that failed the preregistration. |
| 4 | Frozen opportunity-selection rules were changed | HIGH | Spec fidelity | The master invents a 30-minute cooldown but omits BASE’s one-entry-per-episode rule and the frozen composition’s overlap-deduplication rule. A cooldown is not equivalent: it can suppress a new episode while allowing a later duplicate from the same episode. |
| 5 | Branch C contaminates the frozen experiment | HIGH | Dependencies | Evidence §7 freezes “TAPE primary, BASE provisional” and says nothing else is traded during the test. Branch C adds an executable third entry source without excluding it from test entries, daily limits, or acceptance statistics; using it would change the frozen composition and reset the test. |
| 6 | Branch C omits the required mirror trigger | HIGH | Logic | The fallback defines only a pullback stall followed by sell-first. The evidence defines a mirrored bounce-stall trigger for long-first, while the playbook itself requires long-first on DOWN, CHOP, uncertain, Call-Wall, and below-VT states. There is no executable route for those cases without HIRO. |
| 7 | Flow-shutoff is incorrectly global and directionally wrong | HIGH | Logic | The management tree applies the three-minute HIRO scratch to every unpaired leg, although its run/giveback state is defined only for BASE; TAPE has no trough-run contract and Branch C explicitly lacks HIRO. “Before +3 prints” is also wrong for long-first trades, whose completion move is downward. |
| 8 | Missing or invalid SpotGamma inputs have no safe branch | HIGH | Edge cases | The evidence requires long-first only when SG levels are missing, `CW−VT ≤ 0`, or IM is unavailable. The master merely asks whether levels loaded, then leaves `pos` and `room` undefined, allowing BASE or Branch C to open a short without the required fallback. |
| 9 | The trend-state classifier is invented | HIGH | Spec fidelity | The exact `0.10 IM`, 80%-above-VWAP, EMA-stack formula and 10:30/lunch schedule do not appear in the supplied evidence. Evidence only labels trend-state leg order an untested hypothesis. The VWAP series is also unspecified despite the evidence requiring SPY as the declared volume proxy. |
| 10 | TAPE’s range gate is neither faithful nor executable | HIGH | Data contracts | The evidence says “high-range day”; the master silently converts this to “prior-60-min range in top quartile” without defining the reference population, normalization, session boundaries, or whether premarket bars supply the missing history before 10:30. This changes eligibility for the primary branch. |
| 11 | Status tags overstate the evidence | HIGH | Spec fidelity | The master labels the VT veto, late cutoff, flow-shutoff, and negative-flow veto as SETTLED. The evidence calls the VT rule a hypothesis/all-session adaptation, the dip-side late cutoff untested, and the HIRO controls part of a frozen paper experiment. `[OPERATIONS]` is also used despite not being a defined status. |
| 12 | Size-up precedes its stated prerequisite | HIGH | Dependencies | The acceptance test says a pass permits sizing up from one-lot paper, while the next bullet says quote-level SPXW replay is still owed before real size. The order must explicitly require both; spot-touch success alone cannot authorize live sizing. |
| 13 | Frozen veto and re-anchoring boundaries are ambiguous | MEDIUM | Data contracts | The short-flow veto uses an approximate `< −0.8 $B` boundary and gives no numeric mirror for longs. Serial re-anchoring uses an undefined `± c` and unspecified bid, ask, or mark, whereas those choices determine whether the resulting spread is debit or credit. |
| 14 | Position sizing is not executable | MEDIUM | Data contracts | “Normal size” and “half size” have no lot definitions and conflict with the current one-lot paper protocol—half of one contract is impossible. The playbook must distinguish paper-test sizing from later live-sizing policy. |
