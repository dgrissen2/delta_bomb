## Codex Strategy Review — Generic Logic
**Verdict**: FAIL (12 findings)

### Findings

| # | Finding | Severity | Category | Details |
|---|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| 1 | No tested state demonstrates a material, risk-selective edge | HIGH | Conclusion | By raw effect size: prior-hour range is largest—low-to-high quartile raises one-way touch 73.2%→86.4% and round trip 43.2%→68.9%, but adverse >20 bp rises 11.2%→18.9% and >40 bp 2.6%→7.3%. Next is exploratory low-ER within middle-range conditions (+5.6 pp one-way, +7.7 pp round trip), followed by stall/reclaim and marginal ER effects around 1–3 pp. By evidence quality: stall/reclaim has the strongest direct SPX test, but fails its +5 pp adoption threshold and leaves the tail unchanged; range is direct but confounded by volatility; ER/flips lack intervals and holdout; all other turn indicators are indirect or untested. Honest answer: no demonstrated edge; enter on the operating rule. |
| 2 | The tested barriers do not match the requested 3–7 and 15–30 SPX-point outcomes | HIGH | Units / Measurement | A 4 bp barrier equals `0.0004 × SPX`, so its point size changes materially across 2022–2026. The same problem affects the 20/40 bp adverse barriers: they represent 15/30 points only near SPX 7,500. Re-run fixed +3/+5/+7-point targets and −15/−30-point adverse barriers; report bp- and IM-normalized versions only as secondary robustness checks. |
| 3 | Spot touches cannot establish option planting probability or economic edge | HIGH | Outcome Validity | A spot move does not guarantee the required option quote change. Delta, gamma, skew, IV, time decay, bid/ask width, and queue execution all vary along the path. The sell-first and long-first branches are also not economically symmetric merely because SPX up/down touches are similar. No signal should be adopted until a quote-sequenced SPXW state-machine test reports plant rate, achieved credit, stop frequency, net P&L, and ES₉₅. |
| 4 | The reported stall/reclaim effect is arithmetically or estimand-inconsistent | HIGH | Math / Reporting | The displayed pooled rates imply +1.1 pp versus uniform anytime (81.7%−80.6%) and +1.4 pp versus clock-matched anytime (81.7%−80.3%), not the reported +2.58 pp with CI [+1.91,+3.24]. Equal-day weighting could produce a different estimate, but that estimand is not disclosed. Reconcile the calculation and show pooled, equal-day, and clock-matched effects with corresponding intervals. |
| 5 | The stall experiment does not isolate the value of detecting a turn | HIGH | Experimental Design | The trigger bundles an ≥8-point pullback, a five-bar stall, and a running-TPM reclaim, while the control includes all minutes. Its small advantage may come entirely from being armed after a decline. Pre-register an armed-event comparison: immediate entry at arm, stall only, reclaim only, stall∧reclaim, and matched armed non-trigger controls at the same clock, excursion, and volatility state. Permit one event per swing and cluster by session. |
| 6 | The chop conclusion contains a Simpson’s-paradox warning | HIGH | Statistical Logic | Marginally, high ER appears better because it is associated with larger prior ranges. Within every range tier, low ER actually has higher one-way and round-trip rates; the middle-range difference is 82.5% vs 76.9% and 59.0% vs 51.3%. This makes low ER × middle range the best remaining price-only research candidate, but adverse interaction cells, clustered intervals, multiplicity correction, and clean validation are missing. It is a test candidate, not an operating gate. |
| 7 | The “round-trip/adverse ratio is fixed” claim is descriptive, not decision-grade | MEDIUM | Risk Logic | IM-normalized range buckets produce ratios of 3.9→3.6, consistent with volatility scaling rather than a free oscillation state. But the numerator and denominator can overlap, ignore loss magnitude, and are not payoff-weighted. Busy tapes raise round trips 47%→65% while roughly doubling the >40 bp tail; quiet tapes lower both. SG-index states show a possible >40 bp reduction, but SG index is undefined here and is not a price/volume-only feature. No chop bucket currently improves proven economic EV. |
| 8 | No proposed mechanical turn alternative has direct supporting evidence | MEDIUM | Candidate Ranking | Rank by existing evidence: (1) five-bar stall∧running-TPM reclaim—direct SPX evidence, small and non-protective; (2) five-minute reversal bar—price-computable but transferred from SPY and another objective; (3) mature alligator—associated with late entries, not validated as a fade; (4) failed level probe—revert depth 6.8 points versus breach 7.1 gives no tell; (5) failed-low momentum divergence, VWAP-slope flip, and opening-range turn logic—untested for this outcome. Test the first three as mutually exclusive rules on identical armed SPX episodes, entering after the completed bar, with fixed-point outcomes and multiplicity-adjusted session-clustered intervals. Kill any rule that fails an option-economics-derived minimum effect. |
| 9 | “SPX index only” excludes genuine volume and VWAP signals | HIGH | Scope / Feasibility | The cash SPX index has no traded volume. Using SPY volume or VWAP imports a different instrument and violates the supplied scope; calling it SPX evidence would be misleading. Restrict this review to SPX OHLC-derived price states—TPM, ER, ranges, EMAs, opening range, and reversal bars—or explicitly authorize and separately validate a proxy. |
| 10 | The research opportunities do not reproduce the executable strategy | HIGH | Operational Validity | The trigger fires 8.7 times per day and the anytime samples use heavily overlapping 60-minute horizons, while the live process permits one unpaired leg with a 60-minute clock. Repeated observations can count the same swing several times and include trades the strategy could never take. Re-run a chronological state machine that blocks new entries while a leg is open and includes stops, re-anchoring, quote fills, and end-of-day liquidation. |
| 11 | The remaining validation plan cannot establish the claimed tail behavior | HIGH | Validation | The 2025–2026 period is acknowledged as contaminated, and the state tables involve exploratory bins without clustered intervals or multiple-testing control. A 20-session paper log would generate only a handful of >40 bp events and cannot validate a 5–7% tail. Freeze the complete specification now, use all future sessions sequentially, and pre-register sample-size or precision stopping rules based on the adverse-tail and ES₉₅ endpoints. |
| 12 | Kill/keep/test boundaries must be tightened before deployment | HIGH | Decision | **Kill as claimed edges or live gates:** stall/reclaim probability advantage, generic ER/flip chop gates, fixed-barrier range selection, early OR direction calls, raw level probes, alligator fading, and SPY-VWAP under SPX-only scope. **Keep only as conservative controls:** one unpaired leg, no anchor, 60-minute timeout, long-first default, and a price cap—but do not call them optimal until option economics verifies them; stall/reclaim may remain a placement discipline with zero claimed edge. **Test next:** (a) fixed-point matched armed-event turn test; (b) low-ER × middle normalized-range interaction including both adverse tails; (c) mutually exclusive stall, failed-low, and five-minute reversal triggers; and (d) full SPXW quote-level chronological replay. Adoption must require positive net EV after costs with a non-worsening ES₉₅ confidence bound, not an arbitrary probability threshold. |

### Assumptions Audit

| Assumption | Classification | Why It Matters |
|------------|----------------|----------------|
| Four basis points consistently represents a three-point SPX move | Contradicted | The absolute barrier changes with the index level and does not answer the fixed-point question across the sample. |
| Twenty and forty basis points represent 15- and 30-point adverse runs | Contradicted | Historical tail rates are not rates for the requested fixed losses. |
| A qualifying SPX spot touch produces the required option fill | Unsupported | Quote dynamics and transaction costs determine whether the spread can actually be planted. |
| Up/down spot symmetry implies sell-first/long-first economic symmetry | Unsupported | Put skew, gamma, vega, bid/ask behavior, and loss asymmetry differ by branch. |
| The stall cohort isolates the stall/reclaim information | Unsupported | It also conditions on pullback size and differs materially from the anytime control. |
| The +2.58 pp estimate is the same estimand as the displayed probabilities | Contradicted / Unclear | Neither displayed baseline produces that difference; weighting must be disclosed. |
| Higher marginal ER means directional tape improves fills | Contradicted conditionally | Within range tiers, lower ER performs better; range composition drives the marginal result. |
| Higher prior range is a selectable oscillation edge | Unsupported | It mechanically raises both fixed-barrier touches and adverse tails through volatility persistence. |
| A round-trip/adverse probability ratio approximates economic value | Unsupported | It ignores overlapping events, payoff magnitude, option dynamics, and execution costs. |
| VWAP and volume are computable from SPX index data alone | Contradicted | SPX has no native traded volume; SPY would be an external proxy. |
| SPY-derived alligator, reversal, ATR, and VWAP thresholds transfer to SPX | Unsupported | Different instruments and objectives require SPX re-derivation and validation. |
| Repeated overlapping starts represent executable opportunities | Contradicted | The one-leg/60-minute state machine prevents most of those attempts from being taken. |
| The 2025–2026 period is clean out-of-sample evidence | Contradicted | It was used during rule development and is explicitly acknowledged as contaminated. |
| Twenty prospective sessions can validate the 30-point tail | Unsupported | Expected rare-event counts are far too small for a useful tail or ES bound. |
| The 60-minute timeout and +3.5 option-price cap are economically optimal | Unsupported | They are defensible safety controls, but their thresholds have not been derived from quote-level net EV and tail loss. |
| Failure to find early trend direction means no retrace-timing state exists | Fragile | The objectives differ; it narrows plausible candidates but does not falsify turn or conditional-chop signals. |
| No tested rule currently supplies a material price-action edge | Supported within tested definitions | Direct results are sub-threshold or trade higher completion for proportionally larger tails; this does not prove that every possible causal SPX rule has no edge. |
