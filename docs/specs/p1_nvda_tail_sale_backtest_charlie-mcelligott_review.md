## Codex Strategy Review — Charlie McElligott
**Verdict**: FAIL (12 findings)

### Persona Lens

This lens treats wing richness as evidence of forced positioning, not generic mispricing. It notices that a one-sided upside grab, a downside liquidation, and a two-sided post-shock smile create different dealer-gamma feedback loops and fail in opposite directions. Averaging them together can conceal the rare short-gamma loss that determines the book’s survival.

### Findings

| # | Finding | Severity | Category | Details |
|---|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| 1 | Signal and entry timing contain look-ahead bias | CRITICAL | Backtest validity | The trigger is evaluated using day-*t* closing ORATS values, yet entry occurs at 15:55 on day *t*. Those closing values are not demonstrably observable before entry. ThetaData EOD quotes also are not necessarily executable 15:55 NBBOs. Use timestamped pre-entry inputs or execute the next session. |
| 2 | The supplied evidence does not establish fidelity to Pandar’s NVDA book | HIGH | Strategy fidelity | The white paper documents SPX, 20–40 DTE, roughly one-standard-deviation strikes, minute-scale legging, and managed failed placements—not NVDA 5–12 DTE, approximately 4-delta, multiday naked shorts. Put-side conversion is evidenced more clearly than a symmetric call-side buyback program. Results would validate this mechanized variant, not “what Pandar did.” |
| 3 | One trigger conflates mechanically different flow regimes | HIGH | Trigger logic | Wing and kink percentiles can fire during a one-sided spot-up/vol-up grab or a two-sided post-shock smile. The former exposes a short call to a dealer-gamma chase; the latter can trigger both sides and create a short strangle during instability. RR, spot direction, overall front IV, and opposite-wing state are merely tags, so the test pools trades with different causes and failure modes. |
| 4 | The trigger is disconnected from the contract traded | HIGH | Basis risk | A constant-maturity 10-day, fixed-5-delta surface point is not the selected listed 5–12 DTE, 2–6-delta option. Fixed-delta observations also migrate between strikes as spot moves. A measured wing crush therefore need not make the original option profitable or fill its buyback. |
| 5 | Threshold fitting optimizes the wrong outcome and invites overfitting | HIGH | Optimization | Maximizing five-session wing crush does not maximize executable P&L, buyback probability, or tail-adjusted return; spot movement can overwhelm IV compression. The 85/70 baseline, 12-signals-per-year floor, search domain, aggregation statistic, and flat-maximum rule lack economic or statistical derivation. Clustered daily signals further overstate effective sample size. |
| 6 | Instrument and order rules do not define one coherent strategy | HIGH | Instrument design | Approximately 4 delta and the 25%-OTM fallback are materially different exposures, while the acquisition plan does not guarantee historical Greeks. “One strike nearer” creates variable widths rather than a consistent $5 spread. Moreover, a permitted $0.05 sale with baseline `c = $0.10` produces a negative buy limit, making the baseline incompatible with Pandar’s nickel-tail examples. |
| 7 | The fill proxy cannot support a promotion decision | HIGH | Execution | The buy is described as rested immediately but becomes active only next session, excluding same-day reversals central to the mechanism. Close ask misses intraday and gap-open fills that change the position discontinuously from naked to defined-risk. May–August 2026 cannot validate execution across 2023–2025 regimes, and a scalar caveat cannot repair altered fill states or determine whether earlier regime signs flip. |
| 8 | The “free spread” omits its economic opportunity cost | HIGH | Counterfactual | At the proposed fill time, closing the cheaper far short would normally retain more cash than purchasing the nearer option. That foregone cash is the cost of the residual vertical. Without a same-time buy-to-close comparator, later spread payoffs can make an inferior profit-taking choice look free; “median credit kept” is also tautologically `c` under the fill rule. |
| 9 | Naked-call risk and book-level sizing are not controlled | CRITICAL | Tail risk | A put’s zero-price loss is bounded, but a naked call’s loss is not bounded by “NVDA doubles.” The 10%-NLV calculation is per trade and ignores overlapping expiries, simultaneous sides, gap-through-strike moves, volatility-driven margin expansion, forced liquidation, assignment, and accumulated inventory. Excluding PNR and margin survival from pass/fail allows promotion of a strategy that cannot survive its modeled path to expiry. |
| 10 | Split and fee normalization mix incompatible contract units | HIGH | Accounting | Dividing pre-split prices by ten while using a 10× multiplier preserves the dollars of one legacy contract, but that contract represents ten post-split contracts’ exposure. Pooling those observations as “per one contract” overweights pre-split regimes and invalidates account scaling. Fees must likewise be stated per executed leg and normalized to the same exposure unit. |
| 11 | The promotion gates are arbitrary and internally underdefined | CRITICAL | Decision logic | The 60% fill hurdle, half-median-income loss allowance, three-of-four-regime rule, and sign-flip test have no demonstrated economic or statistical basis. “Trailing-12-month median income” is undefined; regimes are unequal and retrospective; criteria are ambiguous between each side and the combined book. A catastrophic call side or one dominant loss regime can therefore be hidden by put income and averaging. |
| 12 | The design cannot falsify signal skill or estimate ruin risk | HIGH | Falsification | Positive results may be ordinary short-vol carry rather than wing-selection skill. Missing controls include unconditional and matched nonsignal sales, randomized dates, immediate verticals, same-option buy-to-close, naked-to-expiry, known Pandar examples, leave-one-shock-out tests, and block-bootstrap uncertainty. Roughly 3.6 years in one preselected stock cannot estimate the ruin probability of repeated 4-delta naked shorts. |

### Blind Spots And Missing Work

- Timestamped, executable signal and quote data—with quote size and order-state assumptions—across every test regime.
- Locked trade reconstructions from Pandar’s actual callouts, with put and call behavior tested separately.
- Portfolio-level PNR, margin, assignment, concurrent-short, and accumulated-spread-inventory simulation.
- Separate flow archetypes using spot direction, RR, opposite-wing state, front IV, and—where available—call/put OI and dealer-gamma concentration.
- Benchmarks, uncertainty intervals, shock-removal tests, and explicit overnight gap/liquidation scenarios.
- A prospective paper protocol with thresholds frozen in advance and promotion decided separately by side and archetype.
