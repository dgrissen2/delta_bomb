# Requirements review — hiro_watch (2026-09-03)

## Round 1 (v1.0) — FAIL, 25 findings, all applied in v1.1

## Codex Plan Review — Verdict: FAIL (18 findings)

| # | Finding | Severity | Dimension | Details |
|---|---------|----------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | `WATCH_HASH` is self-referential | CRITICAL | Logic errors | W8.1 stores `WATCH_HASH` inside the JSON whose canonical bytes are hashed. No ordinary SHA-256 value can be calculated this way. Define canonicalization that excludes or nulls the hash field before hashing. |
| 2 | Candidate-column firewall rejects required W4/W5 inputs | HIGH | Internal consistency | W2.4 and its acceptance criterion prohibit candidates from referencing anything outside W2.2. W4 necessarily uses W2.3 outcomes, entry data, and option-chain paths; W5 uses refusal reasons and replay inputs. Registration would reject valid candidate definitions. |
| 3 | A-DEPTH can clear its count bar with almost no passed evidence | HIGH | Logic errors | W3.4 requires 20 total A signals, not a minimum number of θ*-passed signals or passed signal-days. One passed signal could produce an apparently perfect bound, while zero passed signals makes the rate undefined. Minimum passed counts and clustered days are required. |
| 4 | A-DEPTH failure conditions never produce rejection | HIGH | Logic errors | Only a completion bound ≤0.55 triggers REJECT. A tail loss below −$150, passed expectancy below forgone expectancy, or excessive day concentration merely leaves the candidate INCONCLUSIVE forever. These need explicit rejection or expiry rules. |
| 5 | Promotion does not require positive expectancy | HIGH | Spec fidelity | The Charlie context calls for positive net expectancy. W3.4 requires only passed expectancy greater than forgone expectancy, so a still-loss-making policy can be promoted—for example, −$1 versus −$100 per signal. |
| 6 | A-DEPTH concentration denominator is ambiguous | HIGH | Data contracts | “No single day contributing >25% of them” can mean all A signals or passed signals, while W3.3 reports concentration only among passed signals. These yield different verdicts and must be made identical and explicit. |
| 7 | The clustered lower-bound estimator is contradictory and incomplete | HIGH | Logic errors | W3 calls the statistic a 90% lower bound, while W6.5 uses the 5th percentile: that is either a one-sided 95% bound or the lower endpoint of a central 90% interval. The eligible-session universe, weighting, empty-session handling, one-session behavior, and quantile method are also undefined. |
| 8 | Independent replays do not evaluate the resulting policy | HIGH | Dependencies | Gating an A entry or delaying a W4 fill changes one-leg-at-a-time and three-per-day capacity, potentially enabling or suppressing later setups. W3 and W4 reuse actual entered trades independently and therefore cannot estimate what the promoted sequential engine policy would have done. |
| 9 | Branch-B CREDIT promotion can be vacuous | HIGH | Logic errors | W4.3 permits a verdict after 10 B entries and requires zero baseline fills lost. If none of those entries filled at 0.10, zero fills were lost and promotion can occur without evidence that either candidate credit fills. Require a minimum baseline-fill count and define rejection separately for 0.20 and 0.30. |
| 10 | Refused setups lack a unique identity and deduplication rule | HIGH | Data contracts | `(session_date, minute, branch)` is not guaranteed unique. A setup may emit both `gate_fail` and `skip`, and multiple reasons may describe the same episode; the accounting’s matching `vt_broken` and `gate_fail` counts underscore the risk. W2/W5 need a stable `setup_id`, reason precedence, and deduplication contract. |
| 11 | `levels_invalid` episodes cannot use the specified full exit set | HIGH | Logic errors | W5 requires replay of `levels_invalid` refusals under veto/state-flip exits, but those exits can depend on the unavailable/invalid levels that caused refusal. The spec must define whether such exits are unavailable, reconstructed from another contemporaneous value, or the episode is unscorable. |
| 12 | NaN and unhealthy-feature behavior is undefined | HIGH | Edge cases | W2 permits NaN during warm-up, but W3 does not say whether `r30 = NaN` causes refusal, exclusion, or session failure. Ordinary comparison would classify it as forgone and bias results. Similar rules are absent for invalid health flags, crossed NBBOs, missing Greeks, and non-finite P&L. |
| 13 | Core schemas, units, and enums are incomplete | MEDIUM | Data contracts | Units and nullability are missing for several fields; enums are undefined for `weak_side`, `context_read`, and health fields; `vt_state` lacks equal/invalid states; “completion” is not formally equated to an outcome; and MAE/MFE direction and endpoint are unspecified. |
| 14 | Expiry settlement has an off-by-one boundary and incomplete formula | HIGH | Edge cases | W6.4 settles only when expiry “passes,” leaving an expiring PM-settled bomb in inventory after its settlement close. It must settle on the expiry session after the close. Leg orientation, intrinsic-value formula, $100 multiplier, maximum payoff, and separation from previously banked credit must also be defined. |
| 15 | External mark pulls and mutable feature code defeat determinism | HIGH | Dependencies | W6.2 permits ChainStore network pulls without specifying immutable cache persistence or manifest hashes. W2 also recomputes with the current engine feature module, while `WATCH_HASH` binds neither code nor all replay semantics. Later corrections or code changes can make W9.4 byte identity impossible. |
| 16 | Multi-ledger append has no atomicity or recovery contract | HIGH | Edge cases | One run appends five files, yet W0.4 forbids partial ledgers. A crash after one append leaves an unrecoverable partial session, and the rerun no-op rule does not describe repair. Define staging, validation, atomic commit, and rollback/recovery behavior. |
| 17 | Re-registration and chronological processing are underspecified | HIGH | Dependencies | W8.2 simultaneously names one fixed registration file, refuses overwrite, and requires new file versions without defining filenames or an active-version pointer. The plan also allows arbitrary `watch <date>` order although cumulative inventory, settlement, drawdown, and book rows require chronological processing or deterministic rebuilding. |
| 18 | Tests do not cover the plan’s highest-risk behavior | HIGH | Test adequacy | One fixture per candidate plus a hash test omits hash canonicalization, boundary comparisons, bootstrap empty/single-session cases, duplicate refusals, NaN handling, config/hash mismatch, discovery cutover, W4 verdict variants, expiry settlement, external-mark failures, out-of-order dates, atomic append recovery, and byte-identical reruns. |

## Codex Plan Review — Charlie McElligott
**Verdict**: FAIL (10 findings)

| # | Finding | Severity | Dimension | Details |
|---|---------|----------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | A-DEPTH can promote on a vanishingly small passed cohort | HIGH | Statistical validity | W3.4 requires 20 total A signals across 10 days, but sets no minimum number or number of sessions passing r30 ≤ −4B. A rare threshold could therefore be promoted from only a few qualifying episodes. Require minimum passed signals, minimum passed days, and concentration limits calculated on the passed cohort. |
| 2 | The promotion rule can approve a negative-expectancy gate | HIGH | Decision economics | W3.4 only requires passed expectancy to exceed forgone expectancy. Both may be materially negative, so the gate could “win” by losing money more slowly. Promotion needs positive expectancy or a pre-registered economically meaningful improvement with uncertainty bounds; expectancy and tail failures should also have explicit rejection paths rather than remaining INCONCLUSIVE forever. |
| 3 | Raw r30 depth is not stable across market regimes | HIGH | Flow-regime specification | A fixed −4B threshold may proxy for overall activity, volatility, time of day, expiry effects, or feed scale rather than persistent dealer-flow pressure. W2 records useful regime variables but provides no normalization, feature-scale drift check, or prospective test separating flow depth from continuation versus reversal regimes. |
| 4 | Candidate scoring excludes the asset being accumulated | HIGH | Strategy attribution | W3 and W4 score credits and failed-leg losses but do not assign common-horizon or terminal value to the bombs each candidate would create or reject. A gate could look superior by avoiding failed attempts while also discarding the convexity that supplies the strategy’s intended payoff. Each candidate needs its own shadow inventory and total-book P&L. |
| 5 | CREDIT replay is path-inconsistent after the baseline fills | HIGH | Counterfactual validity | W4.1 preserves recorded flow exits, but once the actual 0.10 trade fills the engine stops managing a lone leg. A counterfactual 0.30 order may remain naked and encounter later scratch, veto, state-flip, cap, or resolution events that were never recorded. All exit conditions must be recomputed minute-by-minute under the counterfactual state. |
| 6 | CREDIT promotion ignores negative-convexity costs | HIGH | Tail-risk governance | W4.3 promotes solely when no baseline fill is lost. It places no bound on added naked-leg duration, MAE, failed-attempt loss, worst loss, or net expectancy, despite W4.2 calculating them. A wider credit can preserve eventual fills while materially worsening path risk; promotion must constrain exposure and tail economics. |
| 7 | B-REFUSED totals are not a deployable portfolio counterfactual | MEDIUM | Exposure accounting | W5 replays every refusal independently even when episodes overlap or would violate the one-unpaired-leg and daily-cap constraints. Summed P&L and naked-short minutes can therefore describe a portfolio the engine could never hold. Label isolated causal replays separately and add a chronological portfolio replay enforcing capacity and competing-signal rules. |
| 8 | Book marks lack executable-quality controls | HIGH | Valuation integrity | W6.2 uses closing mids even though the accounting context already shows adjacent-strike quote inconsistencies. There is no spread-width, crossed-market, staleness, no-arbitrage, or liquidation-side validation for inventory marks. Report conservative executable liquidation alongside mid marks and refuse or flag unhealthy quotes. |
| 9 | Terminal settlement and correlated inventory risk are underspecified | HIGH | Portfolio risk | W1.5 equates settlement with a 16:00 one-minute SPX bar without specifying the authoritative settlement value, timestamp conventions, early closes, or correction handling. Expiry/strike concentration plus a caveat also does not quantify the single correlated downside position; add official-settlement provenance and portfolio payoff/shock scenarios across spot and expiry. |
| 10 | The confidence and registration contracts are internally incomplete | HIGH | Evidence governance | W6.5 calls the bootstrap result a 90% lower bound while using the 5th percentile, and does not define weighting or treatment of sessions with zero qualifying signals. Separately, W8.1 defines WATCH_HASH as the hash of JSON that contains WATCH_HASH, which is self-referential, and omits an explicit watch-code/schema identity. Define the confidence procedure precisely and hash a canonical payload excluding the hash field while binding code and schema versions. |
