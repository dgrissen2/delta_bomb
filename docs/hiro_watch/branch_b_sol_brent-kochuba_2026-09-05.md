## Codex Plan Review — Brent Kochuba (Synthetic Gamma & Microstructure)
**Verdict**: FAIL (6 findings)

| # | Finding | Severity | Dimension | Details |
|---|---------|----------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | The unpaired first leg is structurally short convexity | HIGH | Structural risk | Branch B sells a put before owning the higher-strike put. Until the second leg fills, the strategy is exposed to precisely the path that follows a bullish-flow failure: put value rises as price falls, and deteriorating flow then forces an exit. The four scratches and three veto exits are not incidental noise; they are the mechanism’s adverse state. A 3.5-point cap does not protect against an overnight or intraday gap through the intended exit. Position size, gap loss, and margin demand are unstated. From this lens, that violates the basic requirement not to stand naked short convexity in terrain capable of fast transit. |
| 2 | The payoff is badly asymmetric even before gap risk | HIGH | Trade economics | The three successful entries earned only $10 each, while failures lost $50–$220. At the observed average failed-trade loss of about $93, B needs roughly a 90% fill rate merely to offset failed entries with $10 fills; the observed fill rate is 30%. The 51-minute fill also spent $90 underwater, showing that eventual completion can conceal substantial path risk. This is structural to sell-first execution: upside before pairing is deliberately capped while reversal exposure remains large. |
| 3 | Cash loss alone does not measure Branch B’s complete economics | HIGH | Validation contract | B exists to manufacture a five-point-wide “bomb,” so the three resulting spreads’ marks or settlement payoffs belong in the branch-level result. The brief reports the unpaired-leg cash ledger but does not attribute the bombs’ subsequent value, expiry outcome, or capital usage to B. If they expired worthless, B lost $620; if they paid, the conclusion could differ materially. The owner cannot decide whether B should exist until the complete lifecycle—not merely planting cost—is stated. |
| 4 | Capacity explains the incremental damage, not the original failure | MEDIUM | Attribution | The three capacity-freed trades lost exactly $360, explaining the entire deterioration from v1’s −$260 to a_depth_m4’s −$620. That is a portfolio-capacity artefact: changing A exposed additional B signals even though B’s rules were unchanged. But B was already −$260 on its original seven trades, with three $10 wins and four larger losses. Capacity therefore amplified a pre-existing structural problem; it did not create it. The document should avoid saying B itself became mechanically worse under a_depth_m4. |
| 5 | The entry filter does not control the failure state that matters | HIGH | Microstructure logic | Positive recent HIRO flow may describe the entry moment, but the trade loses when that flow stalls or reverses before the resting buy fills. Seven of ten trades terminated through flow shutoff or veto, demonstrating that the filter selects strong current flow without establishing durable positive-gamma containment. No synthetic-gamma bars, sign-flip proximity, void analysis, or expected-move reachability is supplied. Consequently, the evidence does not show that the short-put interval is protected from dealer-amplified downside. |
| 6 | The sample supports disabling B, not preserving it as a production branch | MEDIUM | Decision | All ten observations are discovery data, while the sole confirmation session produced no trade. There is also a factual timing conflict: the brief says all bombs were planted in the session’s first hour, but fills occurred at 11:34 and 10:45. With no out-of-sample successful trade, incomplete bomb attribution, and seven adverse exits, nothing here establishes a repeatable B edge. The defensible current decision is “B should not exist in production as configured”; capacity should not be reserved for it unless the already-created bombs’ complete outcomes overturn that conclusion. |
