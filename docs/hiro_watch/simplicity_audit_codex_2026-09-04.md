## Codex Plan Review — Architect
**Verdict**: FAIL (8 findings)

| # | Finding | Severity | Dimension | Details |
|---|---------|----------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | The 13-module architecture is not justified | HIGH | Over-engineering | The package decomposes one sequential evening batch into registration, inputs, shadow, panel, ladder, refused, book, stats, verdicts, ledger, report, constants, and CLI modules, creating more contracts than the program needs. Use five substantive modules: `cli.py` (orchestration/reporting), `registration.py` (frozen definition and input validation), `replay.py` (all engine hooks), `scoring.py` (panel, candidates, book, statistics, verdicts), and `ledger.py` (atomic persistence). `__init__.py` and `__main__.py` remain trivial shims. A credible budget is 550–700 production lines plus 350–450 focused test lines; roughly 500 lines total would require dropping meaningful replay/accounting tests, but 1,800 lines is still excessive. |
| 2 | Diagnostic layers multiply work without protecting the experiment | HIGH | Scope control | The θ ladder, isolated CREDIT ladder, up to 20 isolated B replays, candidate panels for every diagnostic run, and diagnostic-versus-portfolio table layers are not load-bearing for frozen definitions, the firewall, or checkpoint verdicts. Keep the registered A threshold −4 only, the four branch-isolated CREDIT portfolio variants, and the three one-rule-disabled B portfolio runs. Retain one baseline 0.10 replay as a fidelity check. Remove isolated B runs, `MAX_ISOLATED`, `entry_filter`, diagnostic layer enums, the θ table, and the standalone `ladder.py`/`refused.py` modules. This reduces the worst case from 29 engine runs to nine. |
| 3 | Cumulative snapshot chains are an unnecessary storage protocol | HIGH | Persistence architecture | Copying every cumulative table into every snapshot creates quadratic storage and requires `current`, `prev_snapshot`, orphan detection, staging-tree rebuilds, pruning policy, prior-inventory derivation, and chain traversal. Store one immutable atomic directory per session containing that session’s rows and a manifest; compute cumulative reports and books by reading session directories in date order. Directory presence supplies the chronology check, and reruns compare only the corresponding daily partition. This preserves atomicity, auditability, no silent skips, and deterministic replay while eliminating most of `ledger.py`. |
| 4 | Code lineage is over-specified and internally fragile | MEDIUM | Reproducibility | `WATCH_HASH`, a mutable append-only `.code` history, per-snapshot `code_hash`, engine config hash, frozen-manifest hash, repeated verification of frozen files, per-table hashes, and nine lineage columns on every row overlap. Pin candidate JSON, watch source hash, engine config/source hash, and per-session input hashes once in each session manifest. Any watch-code change should require a new registration and ledger root; remove `rebind`, `.code`, manifest exceptions during byte comparison, and duplicated lineage columns. This conservative rule is simpler and cannot silently reinterpret prior evidence. |
| 5 | The generic panel framework is larger than the three candidate contracts | MEDIUM | Data model | A 22-column classifier vocabulary, declared `scorer_inputs`, `POST_HOC_PREFIX`, versioned refusal-string map, candidate panels, numerous attribution fields, and a large eligibility enum build a general experiment platform. The actual classifiers need only `r30` for A-DEPTH, branch plus registered credit for CREDIT, and refusal reason for B-REFUSED. Persist the engine event identity, those inputs, trade outcome, `eligible: bool`, and one `exclusion_reason`; retain additional regime columns only in an optional report export. Replace 14 exception subclasses with one `WatchError(code, details)` and reduce verdict state to PROMOTE, REJECT, or INCONCLUSIVE plus explicit blocker reasons. |
| 6 | Book reporting contains a second risk system unrelated to the registered decisions | HIGH | Scope control | Per-candidate mid and liquidation marks, full-chain global caches, row-level `mark_sha`, settlement-source switching and reconciliation, concentration reports, inventory drawdown, and a nine-point shock grid dominate the design. The verdicts require realized cash, settled payoff, and current liquidation MTM only. Use one fixed settlement source, persist only the exact two-leg quote rows used for open bombs with their session-input hash, and keep `UNMARKED` as the sole missing-data state. Remove mid marks, full-chain persistence, concentration, drawdown, shock grids, reconciliation fallback, and duplicate mark hashes. |
| 7 | The outcome attachment contract cannot perform its stated join | HIGH | Contract correctness | `attach_outcomes` says it joins panel rows to trades on `(candidate_id, session_date, trade_id)`, but `trade_id` is only added by that operation and is initially absent or null on the panel. The prose separately implies matching through `(branch, signal_min, episode)`. Define one enforceable join: match on `(candidate_id, session_date, branch, signal_min, episode)`, assert at most one trade per setup, then populate the engine-assigned `trade_id` and `bomb_id`. Without this, entered outcomes can be lost or attached incorrectly. |
| 8 | Recovery and locking contracts contradict each other | HIGH | Operational correctness | Registration §1 and tasks §2 correctly say `rebind` bypasses `require_code_match`, while design §12 says `rebind` begins with that guard; the latter makes recovery from a mismatch impossible. The main-loop pseudocode also dedents all expensive work outside the writer lock while claiming it remains inside. If `rebind` is retained, specify its bypass once; otherwise remove it as recommended above. Show the entire read-current, replay, stage, and commit sequence lexically inside one lock so two invocations cannot derive competing snapshots from the same prior state. |

| 2 | The design invents terminal rejection paths | HIGH | Logic | Requirements expire A-DEPTH only after 40 confirmation A signals ([requirements.md:179](/Users/dgrissen/Dev/delta_bomb/docs/hiro_watch/requirements.md:179)); the design turns any deferral at the 40th confirmation-session checkpoint—including missing count bars, scale drift, or unmarked inventory—into `REJECT-EXPIRED` ([design.md:303](/Users/dgrissen/Dev/delta_bomb/docs/hiro_watch/design.md:303)). | 3 | Settlement has two conflicting authoritative prices | HIGH | Internal consistency | W6.4 first defines settlement as the last regular-hours SPX 1-minute close, then says to prefer a later index-EOD value ([requirements.md:262](/Users/dgrissen/Dev/delta_bomb/docs/hiro_watch/requirements.md:262)). | 4 | The diagnostic layers violate the “run the engine” principle | HIGH | Over-engineering | `ladder.py` copies a separate execution replay and holds exits fixed ([design.md:210](/Users/dgrissen/Dev/delta_bomb/docs/hiro_watch/design.md:210)), while W5 adds up to 20 isolated engine runs ([design.md:220](/Users/dgrissen/Dev/delta_bomb/docs/hiro_watch/design.md:220)). Neither drives verdicts, and both add drift and interpretation risk. Remove `ladder.py`, the A threshold-shape ladder, isolated CREDIT replay, isolated B replays, `entry_filter`, and `MAX_ISOLATED`. Retain the baseline, primary A-DEPTH, four branch-isolated CREDIT variants, and three sole-blocker B portfolio runs: nine engine-authoritative runs. |
| 5 | Cumulative immutable snapshots are an unnecessary event-store | HIGH | Over-engineering | Every session copies every accumulated table into a new snapshot, maintains `current` and `prev_snapshot`, detects orphans, and never prunes ([requirements.md:335](/Users/dgrissen/Dev/delta_bomb/docs/hiro_watch/requirements.md:335), [design.md:318](/Users/dgrissen/Dev/delta_bomb/docs/hiro_watch/design.md:318)). That produces O(n²) stored/written history for a single-user append-only study. | 6 | Code-lineage machinery is much heavier than pre-registration requires | MEDIUM | Over-engineering | The `.code` append log, `active.txt`, `rebind`, full-history rebuild, Parquet byte-identity, and repeated input hashes on every row ([requirements.md:306](/Users/dgrissen/Dev/delta_bomb/docs/hiro_watch/requirements.md:306)) do not strengthen the frozen candidate definitions. | 7 | Calendar and chronology contracts disagree | MEDIUM | Dependencies | Requirements use the engine event calendar plus weekdays and require only earlier countable sessions ([requirements.md:345](/Users/dgrissen/Dev/delta_bomb/docs/hiro_watch/requirements.md:345)); design/tasks introduce `nyse_holidays.csv` as the sole authority and require every disposition row, including noncountable sessions ([design.md:322](/Users/dgrissen/Dev/delta_bomb/docs/hiro_watch/design.md:322)).
