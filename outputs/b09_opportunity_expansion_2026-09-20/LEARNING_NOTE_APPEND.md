**Individual-day robustness.** Existing B09 sector F4 OR SPX was 198/318 = 62.3%
on 121 active dates. Removing each date separately leaves 61.3–63.1%. The largest
winning day supplied 8/198 winners and the top five 34/198. This rejects the narrow
explanation that one spectacular session created the result. It does not narrow
the confidence interval, undo prior selection or establish independent trials.
Partial 2026 H2's 18 signals on seven dates remain too thin for a reliable rate.

**Three separate expansions, no new threshold sweep.**

Each expansion preserves roughly the historical pooled accuracy while adding raw
signals. Whole-date intervals for the accuracy change include improvement and
deterioration; this is not proof of noninferiority. The three expansions were not
combined. Exact date/minute duplicates were collapsed after checking native
outcome/price agreement: seven B05 duplicates, one B07 duplicate.

**Persistence is not stale-data recovery.** At one endpoint T−5…T−1, the same four
ETF identities must have M>1 and falling IV, and those identities must still have
valid falling-IV slopes at T−1. SPX is the separate one-instrument route. All old
qualifiers remain in the raw set. Missing current observations stay unknown; do
not pool votes from different earlier endpoints. The duration was fixed at five
minutes; every endpoint retains its own original causal calibration.

**Execution policy changes the benefit.** Spaced baseline 107/175 = 61.1% becomes
B05 139/230 = 60.4%, persistence 138/235 = 58.7%, B07 142/235 = 60.4%. Persistence
newly keeps 78 entries/48 targets but displaces 18 baseline entries/17 targets.
Replaying the full chronological union is necessary; standalone spaced candidate
counts cannot simply be added to the baseline. No-spacing remains the primary
comparison requested by the user.

**More opportunities are not all new market conditions.** Persistence adds 174
signals but only 18 active dates; B05 adds 19 dates and B07 adds 27. Respectively,
119/174, 48/105 and 24/90 additions occur within 60 minutes of a base entry. Added
signals on entirely new dates are persistence 12/25, B05 10/28, B07 18/36, versus
98/149, 54/77 and 38/54 on already-active dates. The new-date cells are small and
do not establish dependable lower accuracy, but they limit claims of equally
strong expansion into independent contexts. Full-day activity and future nearby
signals are retrospective diagnostics, never entry-time gates.

**B07 preserves the original rule.** This is six-sector falling/downward acceleration
using original quote validity and complete T−35…T−6 observations. It is not the
sparse MAD-F4 transfer. Its older 96/157 result includes 2024; here the common
2025–2026 calendar supplies 91 candidates before deduplication.

**Volume did not earn a new gate.** One fixed RVOL>1 test used five completed SPY
minute volumes divided by the same-clock median over 60 strictly prior sessions.
Nasdaq-venue share volume is a participation proxy, not consolidated volume or
signed flow. Within observed B09 OR, high volume is 84/136 = 61.8%, ordinary volume
92/148 = 62.2%. A high-volume filter would discard 92 observed-volume winners
without improved accuracy. The 34 volume-unknown entries include 22 winners.
Parent coverage is 2,766/3,141: 235 current dates unavailable after June 11 and
140 with incomplete historical support. The modest all-parent pooled advantage
reverses in the same-date/hour comparison under equal-day weighting; do not turn
that into a tuned inverse-volume rule. Price/volatility confounding remains.

**Preserve the corrected new-information ideas.** The latest SPX test was 30-day
ATM acceleration, not short-dated sector or constituent skew. The proposed new
comparison pairs each rising sector ETF or constituent with its own short-dated
call-wing richness, then asks separately whether acceleration helps among cases
with strengthening skew. Match comparable price strength; separate call richness,
put richness and risk reversal; do not mistake changing spot/delta interpolation
or stale prices for new options demand. Dated sector holdings and publication
timing are required; current holdings cannot select historical leaders.

A ten-date measurement pilot is prepared with a fixed seven-day/25-delta proposal,
all eleven ETFs, and a subsequent top-three-per-ETF holdings measurement phase.
This is a budgeted measurement scope, not an optimized trading rule. Existing
8–65DTE/30-day caches do not certify seven-day surfaces. No short-dated data or
outcomes have been collected/tested yet. Actual transaction pressure/order-book
imbalance remains a distinct queued idea, as do term structure, timestamped event
proximity, leadership/concentration controls and validated liquidity/positioning
references. OHLCV does not reconstruct signed order-book flow or dealer gamma.
