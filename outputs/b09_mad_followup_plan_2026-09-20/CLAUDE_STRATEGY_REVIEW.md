### Findings

| 1 | Hit rate is not the objective function; on the +5/−10 barrier, S4 — one of the two rules being transferred — is **worse** than the unfiltered parent | CRITICAL | objective-misspecification | BLOCKING. From the frozen `summary.csv` (pooled, `all`): B09 baseline 1704 target / 685 adverse / 751 neither → (1704×5 − 685×10)/3141 = **+0.532 pts/signal**, win:loss 2.49. `signed_m1_b4` (S4) 139/61/35 → **+0.362 pts/signal**, win:loss **2.28**. S4 raises the hit rate +4.9pp *by raising adverse_first from 21.8% → 26.0%* and cutting "neither" 23.9% → 14.9%. F4 is +0.612 vs +0.532 — a +0.08 pt gain that reverses if un-barriered trades average better than ≈+0.9 pts, which is unmeasured. The ranking inverts completely: the five-sector rules the proposal explicitly refuses to transfer are the only strong cells (F5 +1.172, S5 +1.062 pts/signal). Neither FINDINGS nor the proposal reports the adverse/neither split, and evaluation-contract items 5 and 7 do not require it for the transfer cells. Fix before running: report adverse_first/neither/ambiguous per cell, declare the exit convention and P&L for "neither", and re-rank all four B09 cells on expectancy. On expectancy evidence, S4 should not be transferred at all. |
| 2 | Undisclosed prior search on the exact transfer targets, in the same artifact the proposal reads its parent table from | CRITICAL | prior-selection | BLOCKING. `branch_b_iv_full_2024_2026_2026-09-19-v1/comparison.csv` holds **104 rules × 13 variants × 3 modes × 7 periods** — B05 and B07 have already been swept with ~103 IV rules. On the same 2025–2026 above-VT population the closest acceleration analogues give: **B05** baseline 55.6% → `guarded_100_accelerating_6` 57.6%, `original_accelerating_6` 57.7%, `original_falling_6` **55.6%** (zero uplift), `original_falling_8` **52.2%** (negative); **B07** baseline 56.1% → `guarded_100_accelerating_6` 66.2%, `original_accelerating_6` 62.6%, `original_falling_8` 69.0%. The proposal declares only the 48-cell B09 search as prior selection. This both understates multiplicity and suppresses directly relevant already-computed evidence that argues **against** B05 (its designated "high-N challenger", whose falling-family analogues are null/negative) and **for** B07. Disclose this table, and justify B05 against it or drop B05. |
| 3 | S4's own uplift interval includes zero under every reporting policy, and the proposal never says so | HIGH | evidence-omission | BLOCKING for how S4 is presented. `uncertainty.csv`, `signed_m1_b4`: all-entries uplift **−1.55 to +11.35**, first-per-day **−5.40 to +16.21**, spaced60 **−0.29 to +15.77**. `falling_m1_b4` first-per-day is **−1.08 to +20.30**, and its all-entries lower bound is **+0.013pp**. All unadjusted for 48 correlated cells. The proposal presents S4 and F4 as established "fixed rules to carry forward" and quotes only point estimates; FINDINGS discloses the F4 +0.01pp figure but the proposal drops it and never mentions S4's intervals at all. The document is transferring an effect whose existence is not established at its own declared uncertainty level. |
| 4 | The B07 transfer is predictably underpowered and no N floor is declared | HIGH | statistical-power | BLOCKING. B07's 2025–2026 above-VT parent is 415 entries. B09 retention was 7.5% (S4) / 6.0% (F4); prior analogues on B07 retained 15.7–21.9% at looser settings. Scaling the observed B09 strictness ratios (S4 is 2.9× stricter than `original_accelerating_6`; F4 is 8.8× stricter than `original_falling_6`) gives **B07 pooled N ≈ 18–31, ≈6–10 per completed half** — the exact regime the B09 study itself declared uninformative ("for thresholds 2, 3 or 4, the largest pooled N is only 26"; "two and one signals … cannot establish a continuation"). Item 9 forbids changing an N floor after results, but no N floor is ever set, so a 25-signal three-positive-halves result would be published as a "stable" transfer. Declare minimum pooled and per-half N for "insufficient evidence" before running. |
| 5 | The declared success criterion is uncalibrated against the declared cell budget | HIGH | multiplicity | BLOCKING. "Positive observed uplift in each of three completed halves" passes with P ≈ 0.5³ = 0.125 per cell under the null. With **8 declared cells** (4 primary + 4 sign controls), expected null passes ≈ 1.0; P(≥1 pass) is substantial even before accounting for the nesting (S4 ⊃ F4, control ⊃ primary) that makes the cells correlated. This is the same criterion that already selected 4 of 48 B09 cells. The proposal's own guard — "Do not change an accuracy floor, N floor, or half-year set after seeing results" — does not help, because the criterion was never calibrated in the first place. Pre-register one primary cell, or state that any pass is hypothesis-generating only, and pre-declare the reading for 0, 1 and ≥2 passes. |
| 6 | The magnitude-attribution control is applied only where power is weakest and omitted where it is free and strongest | HIGH | missing-control | BLOCKING. The `a<0` control (equivalently M>0, since M = −a/(1.4826·MAD) and MAD>0) is specified "per parent" for B07/B05 only. On B09 the M scores, b2 values, memberships and outcomes are already frozen in `memberships.parquet` / `sector_at_entry.parquet` / `events_with_outcomes.parquet` — the M>0/breadth-4 control costs **zero new data** and has 3,141 parent entries versus ~415 on B07. FINDINGS confirms "No lower thresholds or smaller breadth values were searched," so k=0 is genuinely new information. Without it, the original B09 result — the sole motivation for this entire follow-up — never receives its magnitude attribution, and the controls that are run land at N≈25 where they cannot resolve anything. Run the two sign controls on B09 first. |
| 7 | The stated objective ("improve N") is structurally unachievable by the round as designed | HIGH | internal-contradiction | B05 filtered N ≈ 1537 × 6–7.5% ≈ 80–120; B07 ≈ 18–31. Both are **below** the existing B09 rule's 188/235. Item 10 forbids summing overlapping signals across parents and defers any combined-execution policy to a future round. So no outcome of this round can raise retained N above the status quo. Yet "the goal is to improve N" is the stated reason for rejecting the five-sector rules (which finding 1 shows are the only economically strong cells) and for rejecting B03. The document concedes this in one sentence ("a portability check, not a fresh out-of-sample proof") but its entire selection logic rests on an N gain the round cannot deliver. Either reframe the round as a pure portability/overfit test — in which case the N argument stops driving the choices — or bring the combined-overlap policy into this round so N is actually measurable. |
| 8 | No within-date or within-hour matched control; the uplift may be date or clock selection rather than an entry-level signal | HIGH | missing-control | BLOCKING (cheap). S4 fires on **100 of 178** active B09 dates, F4 on 95 — this is not a rare-regime filter, it is a >50%-of-dates selector. The MAD baseline is built per ETF **and hour block**, so qualification is hour-conditional by construction. Contract items 7–8 require comparison to the full parent and to the yes+no measurable population, but never to the parent **restricted to the same dates**, or to matched date×hour cells. That single diagnostic separates "the rule picks better entries" from "the rule picks better days/hours," every input is already frozen, and it is not in the plan. |
| 9 | The SPX rule is not selectivity-matched to F4, so the disagreement test conflates different information with different strictness | HIGH | non-comparable-cohorts | F4 requires 4 simultaneous exceedances across 11 instruments and qualifies ~6% of entries. `M_SPX > 1 AND b2_SPX < −1e−12` is a **single-instrument, breadth-1** condition; by construction of a MAD-scaled score a one-sided >1-MAD deviation fires on a large fraction of windows. The pre-declared OR test will therefore add a large group of near-base-rate entries — precisely the outcome the proposal names as failure ("If added entries dilute accuracy … increasing N alone is not success"). Pre-registering a test whose result is predictable from the construction is not a test. Also, the sector analogue of breadth-1 was never evaluated (FINDINGS: "No lower thresholds or smaller breadth values were searched"), so there is no matched sector reference. Fix: report the SPX qualification rate before outcomes, and either frequency-match the SPX threshold to F4's ~6% or add the free sector breadth-1 cell as the matched comparator. |
| 10 | Single-instrument missingness has no redundancy analogue, and no acceptable unknown rate is declared | MEDIUM | data-integrity | The 11-sector rule tolerates gaps via bounded counting — XLRE is available on only **1,906 of 3,141** entries (60.7%) and the rule still resolves. A one-instrument SPX rule has no fallback: any unmeasurable window makes the entry unknown outright, and the "sector definite no / SPX yes" cell — the proposal's declared key group — requires both legs measurable simultaneously. The plan requires unknown groups be kept separate but sets no expected or acceptable SPX unknown rate and no rule for interpreting the cross-classification if, say, 20% of entries are SPX-unknown. Related: the running SPX collector requests **09:30–14:29 ET** (derived through 12:59 on early closes), matching the sector measurement hours — confirm and report entry-minute coverage parity rather than assuming it. |
| 11 | B03/staircase is dropped on the one criterion where it is weakest, discarding the cleanest overfit test available | MEDIUM | missing-falsification | B03 (five-minute staircase) is the near-replicate of B09 (native-minute staircase). A rule that works on B09 but fails on its own coarser-timeframe twin is strong evidence of entry-structure-specific overfit — that is a **falsification diagnostic, not an N play**, so rejecting it "because the current objective emphasizes N" applies the wrong criterion to the wrong candidate. B03's 2025–2026 parent (189 entries) is the same order as B07's 415, which the proposal does keep. Optional but high-information: retaining B03+F4 as a declared falsification cell costs one cell. |
| 12 | "Unknown" groups score at or above the "yes" groups, undermining the premise that the frozen cutoffs are the transferable object | MEDIUM | threshold-validity | S4: yes 139/235 = 59.1%, **unknown 130/212 = 61.3%**, no 1435/2694 = 53.3%. F4: yes 61.2%, **unknown 111/183 = 60.7%**, no 53.4%. The near-miss group performs at least as well as the qualifying group, i.e. the discrimination is not happening at the M>1 / breadth-4 boundary — consistent with a broad regime effect rather than a threshold effect. At breadth 5 the pattern inverts (unknown 52.1% / 50.6%), which is itself evidence of instability across adjacent cells. FINDINGS notes the unknown rates as an opportunity cost; neither document draws the inference about threshold validity, and the transfer plan freezes exactly these cutoffs. Report yes/unknown/no side by side per transfer cell and treat a yes ≈ unknown result as a failed threshold claim. |
