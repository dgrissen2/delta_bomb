## Claude Strategy Review — Generic Logic
**Verdict**: CONDITIONAL PASS (9 findings)

### Findings

| # | Finding | Severity | Category | Details |
|---|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| 1 | The data shows the filters stopped helping. It does not show that the filters make B06 worse, but the CIO reading can be read that way. | MEDIUM | Overclaim / interpretation | The CIO text says "All four frozen IV requirements did worse" and cites the original rule's new-100 interval of −11.0 to −0.4. I recomputed from `event_ledger.csv` and compared qualifying signals with non-qualifying signals on the same day (Mantel-Haenszel, date strata). In the new 100 that contrast is about zero: original −1.2 pp [−9.5, +7.4], midpoint +0.3, guarded_100 +2.8, guarded_50 +6.1. Most of the −5.7 pp raw uplift (about −4.1 pp) comes from which dates the filter fires on. It fires more on dates where plain B06 does badly (corr(qualifying share, day hit rate) = −0.18 for new 2025 dates, −0.22 for new 2026). The one interval that excludes zero is 1 of 20 shown, and its upper end is only −0.4. With a month-block bootstrap it becomes [−11.9, +0.3]. The data supports "no reliable effect within a day." It does not support "harmful," and it gives no basis for a reverse rule. The document should say this explicitly. |
| 2 | The document calls the result a failed replication but does not explain why the result flipped so much, which was the main question. | MEDIUM | Missing analysis | The flip is larger than date-level noise alone would predict. Original uplift minus new-100 uplift is +14.0 pp [3.2, 24.5], P(≤0) ≈ 0.004 with independent date bootstraps. The same-day contrast fell from +14.1 [0.8, 27.5] to −1.2 [−9.5, 7.4]. **Backed by evidence:** (a) The original 50 dates were used for about 13 earlier B06 studies, so the +8.1 was chosen after the fact and its own interval already included zero. A simple test for difference between cohorts ignores that selection, so a large drop is expected. (b) Date composition in the new data (Finding 1). (c) The mechanism changed. In the original 50, the whole gain came from fewer "neither" outcomes (−12.4 pp [−22.2, −2.9]); the "adverse first" rate did not improve (+1.5 pp). In the new 100 the timeout reduction disappeared (+2.1) and "adverse first" went up (+5.8 [0.3, 11.6]). **Hypotheses only:** the condition became more common (it fired on 36% of new-2025 signals vs 25% originally) and drifted toward weak days; or the classification is mostly quote noise (Finding 8). The document should present these separately, labelled as evidence or hypothesis. |
| 3 | The CIO says the result "is not explained solely by adding 2025." The 34 new 2026 dates are too few to show that. | MEDIUM | Underpowered subgroup | New-2026 original-rule uplift is −4.9 pp with a date-bootstrap interval of [−16.3, +6.0]. The gap between the original 50 and new 2026 is [−1.4, +27.8], which includes zero. The claim that "every IV row trails" counts nested, overlapping rules as if they were four confirmations; they are about one. Year and cohort are also fully tangled: every 2025 date is new and every original date is 2026. So the year tables cannot separate "2025 regime" from "fresh versus reused dates." The statement should be softened to: "the new 2026 dates provide no support, but cannot distinguish partial persistence from failure." |
| 4 | Plain B06 fell from 63.9% to 55.7%, and the document never explains why. | MEDIUM | Baseline / regime | The drop is almost entirely 2025 timeouts under a fixed-point +5 target. "Neither" was 37.1% on new-2025 dates vs 22.5% in the original 50; "adverse first" was similar (10.9% vs 13.6%). New-2026 baseline is 62.9%, close to the original. I measured the 09:30–10:04 SPX range, which is known before every entry. Median range was 21.7 pts on new-2025 dates vs 27.1 originally; the top-third range bucket held 16% vs 48% of signals. Hit rate by range bucket: low 44%, mid 60%, high 72%. Within the same bucket, the original 50 and 2025 are matched in the low and mid buckets. The filter still does no better inside any bucket in the new data. So volatility regime explains the baseline drop, not the filter's reversal. Saying so blocks two wrong readings: "B06 degraded" and "the IV failure is just low volatility." |
| 5 | The date bootstrap treats dates as independent, but the new dates are bunched by regime. | LOW | Uncertainty | 34 of the 100 new dates fall in Sep–Oct 2025 and 14 in Dec 2025. Month-block interval: [−11.9, +0.3]; week-block: [−11.5, −0.1]. The direction holds: dropping any one month leaves the point estimate between −4.4 and −8.0, and dropping any one date between −6.6 and −5.2. Interval-based claims should still be treated as borderline. The combined-150 bootstrap also does not keep the original/new split in its resampling. |
| 6 | The failed-replication conclusion covers a narrower set of days than the headline implies. | LOW | Scope / sampling | The population is days that opened above VT and had a matching same-day pre-open note. May–Jul 2025 is missing mainly because notes are missing (56 of 64 dates), not because of the regime; the post-tariff rebound is therefore absent. March 2026 has no eligible dates (21 opened below VT). The original 50 used all 8 eligible Feb 2026 days. The conclusion applies to above-VT opens in Feb and Aug–Dec 2025 and Jan–Aug 2026 excluding March, entries 10:05–14:25, a 60-minute horizon and the +5/−15 target. SAMPLING.md notes some of this; the CIO sentence "did not carry to the additional data" should carry the scope too. |
| 7 | The uplift measure compares qualifying signals with the full baseline, which dilutes the effect and mixes in coverage and time-of-day effects. | LOW | Metric design | The baseline includes the qualifying signals themselves plus the unknown ones. Guarded rules leave most early parents unknown (69% of new ≤10:30 parents for guarded_100), and those have the highest base rates. Adjusting for time of day, guarded_50 moves from −5.2 to −3.2 in the new 100 and from +12.0 to +15.4 in the original 50. The sign is unchanged. Bounds on unknown states are robust: new-100 guarded_50 lies between 50.5% and 55.8% against a 55.7% baseline. Recommend reporting qualifying vs definite-no and same-day contrasts alongside uplift as main diagnostics. |
| 8 | An earlier finding about measurement noise is not carried into this document. | LOW | Omitted evidence | The earlier acceleration study (`b06_iv_acceleration_increment_2026-09-17`) recorded that none of 1,604 qualifying sector/event acceleration signs cleared its conservative quote envelope. If the classification sits inside quote noise, failing to replicate is the expected outcome, and more IV-threshold tuning would not help. This is a hypothesis. It can be checked on cached data by reclassifying with IV perturbed inside the bid/ask range and measuring how many qualifying labels flip. |
| 9 | The summary gives the original range as "72–76%," but one row was 69.6%. | LOW | Factual accuracy | The four original rates were 72.0, 69.6 (midpoint), 71.0 and 75.9. The range should read 69.6–75.9%. Every other number I checked reconciles: 1,040 parents / 149 active dates, 18 parents at 10:05, 35 missing panels, unknown-state row sums, all interval endpoints, and the 34-date subtraction. |
